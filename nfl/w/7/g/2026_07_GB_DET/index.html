The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

Detroit won 20-17 on a 22-yard Bates field goal with one second left, Green Bay covered +3.5, and the total cleared the under by 18.5 points. Both teams moved the ball — Green Bay 0.164 EPA/play between the 20s, Detroit 0.089, 131 combined snaps at a pace above the league pair — and neither could finish: three touchdowns and three made field goals from ten combined red-zone trips, with a missed 26-yarder, a blocked 35-yarder and four failed fourth downs attached. The margin came from Green Bay's flags: three contact fouls on Detroit's possessions, the last a roughing on Karl Brooks at Q4 2:45 that turned 2nd-and-8 at the DET 27 into field-goal range in one snap.

How it unfolded

Green Bay opened with a 62-yard drive that died on a delay of game in field-goal formation and a missed 26-yarder (Q1 10:26). Detroit answered in six plays and 80 yards, with 28 to St. Brown and 24 to Jameson Williams before the 16-yard touchdown. Green Bay's response drive was the game in miniature: a 4th-and-2 conversion to Kraft for 22 (+4.51 EPA) and a 14-yard touchdown to Kraft on 3rd-and-9. St. Brown's 40-yard catch with :53 left set up a 40-yard field goal.

The second quarter produced 86 plays' worth of nothing. Green Bay's 13-play, 73-yard drive — including a 4th-and-3 conversion to Watson for 28 — ended on 4th-and-4 at the DET 7 (-3.01 EPA). Detroit's 12-play, 85-yard answer ended with Gibbs fumbling at the GB 6 with :30 left.

The third quarter decided the shape. Goff's aborted snap at his own 41 (Q3 14:16, -5.87 EPA, the largest single swing of the day) gave Green Bay the ball at the DET 42, and Jacobs' 25-yard catch made it 14-10. Detroit took it back on a 10-play drive capped by Williams' second score, then stalled on 4th-and-5 from the 6 at 1:09. In the fourth, Green Bay's 13-play, 56-yard drive produced a 43-yard tie, Detroit's 4th-and-1 from its own 24 was stuffed for -2, Green Bay's 35-yard go-ahead attempt was blocked, and the Lions covered 57 yards in eight plays — 15 of them free on the roughing flag.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The market card was perfect and the team totals were not: 26.0 and 29.5 projected, 17 and 20 delivered. The yards arrived (GB 274 passing on 264.5, DET 119 rushing on 110.1); the finishing did not. Green Bay's -0.629 red-zone EPA/play on 11 snaps and Detroit's -0.438 on 14 are the entire miss. The one structural error was volume shape — 45 GB attempts against 36.1, the only 'big' flag, driven by 55 of 67 snaps spent trailing with a run game at -0.238 EPA/rush. Detroit's 303 passing yards on 32 attempts was the inverse: the blend put DET at 6.91 Y/A splitting its 7.78 offense against GB's 5.95 defense, and the game landed on Detroit's number with 6-of-9 deep completions. The GB backfield projection was simply wrong — three projected backs inactive, Jacobs unlisted at 52% of snaps.

The plan against the game

The plan nailed the architecture and missed the scoreboard. Pace was called fast and came in at 131 plays; both leans held (Detroit 50.0% pass against a 60.6% season rate, Green Bay 70.8% against 65.6%); Detroit led, fed Gibbs 29 carries and Green Bay threw 45 times chasing — the favorite path as written. The two failures were paired and instructive. The plan's unders on Goff and St. Brown both assumed volume suppression would suppress production; volume was suppressed (32 attempts, below its own 32-35 band) and Goff still threw for 303 and St. Brown for 153, because Detroit's yards come in chunks, not snaps. And the favorite path's promise — 'touchdowns rather than field goals' — inverted completely.

Player by player

Golden is the instructive line: 12 targets, 165 air yards, a 36% share, and 39 receiving yards with zero YAC and -8.01 EPA. The role call was right; deep-target yardage is a coin flip. Love's 274 on 45 attempts with -0.5 CPOE was volume without efficiency; he faced one sack and six hits, a 13.0% pressure rate below league. Jacobs took the lead back role and the receiving-back role nobody had priced. On the other side, St. Brown's 9-153 on 11 targets came without a chasing script, Williams turned nine targets into 91 and two scores, and LaPorta's 97.1% snap share bought two targets. Gibbs' 116 yards hid -6.64 rushing EPA, four stuffs and the two short-yardage failures that nearly cost Detroit the game.

The defenses

Charting is snap counts only here — no pressure or coverage data — so the proxies carry it. Green Bay generated one sack and one QB hit on 33 Goff dropbacks (6.1%), which is the same picture as week 3 against Atlanta, and gave up four 20-plus throws. It also held Gibbs to 3.6 a carry with four stuffs and stopped two fourth downs, which was not the pregame expectation of a front missing bodies. Detroit, faced with a 10.2 aDOT and 12 deep attempts, surrendered six explosives and three fourth-down conversions worth +13.3 EPA and still allowed 17 points — the bend-don't-break profile its four 'red zone defense' tags describe, confirmed again at 1-of-4.

What the model should take from this

Three things. Detroit's passing production is detached from Detroit's passing volume — price Goff and St. Brown off explosive-play rate, not attempts. Green Bay's engine input for its own run game (2.87 YPC, 1.70 before contact) should push its pass rate higher in any trailing projection; 45 attempts was not a surprise given the script. And the red-zone collapse on both sides, plus two missed kicks inside 36 yards, is noise that happened to decide a side — the field-EPA numbers, not the trip counts, are what should carry into next week.
