The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

Pittsburgh won 16-10 as a three-point favorite, covering, and the game landed 17.5 points under a 43.5 total. Both offenses moved the ball — Pittsburgh at +0.192 EPA per play between the 20s, New Orleans at +0.203 — and neither could finish: seven combined red-zone trips produced two touchdowns, three Boswell field goals, one Carlson field goal, a missed 52-yarder and an interception at the Pittsburgh 11. The structural cause sat in the pre-game inputs: New Orleans could not run (18 yards on 17 carries), so it could neither control the game nor finish inside the 20, and every one of its 57 snaps came while trailing.

How it unfolded

The first meaningful snap told the story. On 2nd-and-7 from his own 42 at Q1 14:20, Warren went up the middle for 29 against a front that had been tagged for the run in weeks 1, 2 and 4; Boswell's 28-yarder followed and Pittsburgh never trailed again. New Orleans answered at the field but not at the goal line: an 11-play, 42-yard drive punted, then a 6-play, 62-yard drive that lived entirely on Shough's 49-yard shot to Barion Brown (Q2 13:17, +2.96 EPA) and ended in a 38-yard field goal.

Pittsburgh's only touchdown drive was one play long in substance — Rodgers to Metcalf for 51 on 3rd-and-14 at Q2 8:34, the biggest EPA (+5.09) and WPA (+0.209) swing of the game, turning a punt into first-and-goal and a 4-yard score to Darnell Washington. The Saints' best stretch followed: a 12-play drive with 4th-and-2 and 4th-and-1 conversions, the second a 1-yard touchdown to Vele, tying it at 10 with 1:45 left — their high-water mark at a 0.457 win probability. Pittsburgh took it straight back with a no-huddle two-minute drive (Warren 15 on 3rd-and-1) and a 47-yard field goal at the gun.

The second half was two long Saints drives that produced nothing. The first went 14 plays and 68 yards to the Pittsburgh 10 before Shough's short right throw was intercepted by Payton Wilson (Q3 7:43, -6.15 EPA). Pittsburgh answered with 13 plays and 7:18 for a 39-yard field goal. The second went 10 plays and 51 yards into a missed 52-yarder. From there Pittsburgh ran the clock: a three-and-out ruined by a Chase Young sack, then a nine-play, 5:52 drive to end it, the key snap a Rodgers scramble for 8 on 3rd-and-6 at Q4 2:49.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The grade is a B (0.772) carried by the market score of 1.000 and the player score of 0.883; the team score (0.586) is almost entirely New Orleans' running game. The projection gave the Saints 26.1 carries for 100 yards. They got 17 for 18 — the worst single line in the dossier (score 0.09) — because the run was both unprofitable (rush EPA -0.526/play, 6.2% success, Miller stuffed twice) and situationally impossible: a team that never leads does not get clock-killing carries. The engine's blended 3.83 ypc already flagged this as the softest spot in the matchup, so the direction was right and only the depth of the hole was missed.

Pittsburgh's 16 points against 23.2 is a finishing miss, not a movement miss. The Steelers ran 52 plays outside the 20 at +0.192 EPA and 10 inside it at -0.341, with sacks on 3rd-and-6 at the NO 25 (Q2 0:23) and 3rd-and-10 at the NO 14 (Q3 0:50). The pass-attempt and carry misses are pure script: the blended 0.587 pass rate beat the team's own 0.66 season mark at predicting the actual 0.548, which is the blend working as designed.

The plan against the game

The plan's favorite path reads like a transcript: Pittsburgh ahead early, Warren at 18 carries against the soft front, Rodgers at 30 attempts, Boswell kicking three, the Saints chasing. Pace held (120 plays vs 121.5 league), both leans held, and the calls went 7-4. The one read it got backwards cost it twice. It treated a one-score deficit as insufficient to generate chase volume, so it took Shough under 224.5 (he threw 38 times for 278) and Olave under 5.5 catches (10 targets, six catches) — in a game where the Saints spent 100% of their snaps behind. And it described Metcalf as a catch-and-run, target-count play; he was the 110-air-yard deep role, which is why six targets became two catches for 56.

Player by player

Warren was script-proof as billed: 18-101 with two 10-plus runs, plus 3 catches for 36 on negative air yards, 137 combined. Dowdle, questionable all week, was the surprise — 9 carries and 6 targets on 43.8% of snaps, 51 YAC, taking the checkdown role the model assigned to receivers. On the other side, Kamara's carry count matched the projection exactly (6) and produced 5 yards, while his receiving line (7 targets, 7 catches, 57 yards, +4.21 EPA) blew past it: in a chase script he is the passing-down back, not a runner. Olave's ten targets produced 56 yards and -1.68 EPA at a 4.3 team aDOT. Vele's 5-for-5 for 54 with the fourth-down touchdown was the efficiency outlier; his 89.5% snap share is the repeatable part. Barion Brown's 3-for-57 on 21% of snaps is one play and should be treated as such.

The defenses

Charting for pressure and coverage has not landed, so read it through proxies. Pittsburgh generated 2 sacks and 5 QB hits on 41 dropbacks (12.2%), below its own season rate, and allowed 30 completions — but at a 4.3 aDOT with only two 20-plus gains, which is the shape of a defense conceding the field and holding at the 20 (NO red-zone EPA -0.419 on 13 snaps). New Orleans faced a 37.1% blitz rate from Pittsburgh's perspective and got home three times on Rodgers, twice via Chase Young; its run defense gave 4.93 a carry and three explosive runs, the recurring facet.

The blueprint

New Orleans is beaten the same way every week: run at them, keep the ball, and make them finish drives with no ground threat. That requires a real rushing offense — Pittsburgh brought 4.58 season ypc and converted 10 of 16 third downs for 32:03 of possession. Pittsburgh is beaten by explosive passing (tagged weeks 3, 4, 5) and by pressure on Rodgers (4, 5 and 5 sacks in weeks 2, 4, 6); the Saints produced the pressure but had only three deep attempts to work with, which is the trap — the weakness was real and the opponent's 4.3 aDOT could not reach it.

What the model should take from this

Two adjustments. First, for New Orleans, the trailing threshold is 'behind at all,' not 'behind by two scores' — and their passing yardage must be priced separately from their team total, because 278 yards and 10 points is now their signature. Second, Pittsburgh's finishing has stalled twice (week 3, here) with field goals piling up behind positive field EPA; that is worth a small, persistent haircut on their touchdown props and team total even when the yardage projects fine. The red-zone bounce on the Saints' side — a 76.5% season rate going 1-for-3 with a missed 52-yarder — is a small sample and should not move their inputs.

Completions and passing yards decouple from points when aDOT is under 5. Shough went 30/38 for 278 at a 4.3 aDOT with 143 YAC and the Saints scored 10. Buy the yardage/reception props, fade the anytime-TD and team-total side.

A pass-catcher's target count and his receiving yards can move opposite ways in the same chase script. Olave's targets rose to 10 (projected 6.9) while his yards stayed at 56 and his receiving EPA was -1.68; Metcalf saw 6 targets with 110 air yards and caught 2.
