The read

Cincinnati is a field-goal road favorite in a 46.5-point divisional game, and the lever is Lamar Jackson's absence — ruled out Saturday, with Rapoport reporting Sunday morning he is tracking for Week 8.

more

Tyler Huntley makes a third straight start into the one unit Cincinnati actually has: a pass defense ranked 4th in EPA allowed, 2nd in red-zone EPA and 2nd in red-zone touchdown rate per trip. That same Bengals defense is 30th in yards per carry allowed and 25th in rush EPA. So Baltimore's path is Derrick Henry and short completions, and its finishing is the part most likely to fail — the profile is field position into field goals, not touchdowns. Cincinnati's own problem is the mirror: it cannot run (3.63 yards per carry, negative rush EPA in four of five weeks) into a Ravens front allowing 3.82 per carry, 9th. Burrow has thrown 32-plus times in every game. Expect a pass-leaning Bengals offense attacking a secondary that has given up four or more explosive passes in three of six weeks, with Humphrey rating in the 12th percentile of corners.

Why

Baltimore's two Huntley starts produced 201 and 165 passing yards at 6.3 and 5.3 per attempt, and our Week 6 review found the Ravens throwing 72.5% of snaps while leading because the run stopped working without a quarterback run threat.

more

Here the run should work — Cincinnati is the softest front he has met since Week 1 — but the red zone is a wall, which is where Henry's touchdown price and Baltimore's kicker diverge from the yardage. On the Cincinnati side, volume is the dependable half: Higgins has led the air-yards share in three of five weeks, and Chase's reception rung is set at a number he has cleared once all year.

What would change it

Any late news on Jackson (the designation is filed, but a surprise activation rewrites both sides).

more

Baltimore's interior line — Stanley did not practice at all. And Cincinnati's three veteran defensive linemen listed as rest-day DNPs: if Lawrence, Allen and Hill are genuinely limited, the run-defense fade gets worse, not better.
