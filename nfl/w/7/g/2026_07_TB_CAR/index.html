The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

Tampa Bay, a three-point road dog, won 43-13 and never trailed for a single second. It did it with 11 pass attempts — eleven — and 49 carries for 258 yards, which is the inverse of the projection in every respect that matters (33.4 attempts, 26.6 carries, 20.8 points). The spread and the total both cashed for the model while the winner call burned, and the F grade (0.354) is almost entirely the Tampa team block: five lines, five zeroes. Carolina's side was nearly exact on yardage (88 rush yards against 88.7 projected, 210 pass yards against 249.4) and ten points light on scoring, which tells you the misses were different species. Tampa's was a script collapse. Carolina's was turnovers and finishing.

How it unfolded

The second snap of the game was Irving right tackle for 15, and the first drive was 14 plays, 70 yards, 7:38, a touchdown, and the entire thesis. Carolina punted, Tampa punted, and then David Moore muffed the punt at his own 14 with :59 left in the first quarter — a +5.10 EPA play that became a 22-yard field goal and 10-0. Two drives later Al-Quadin Muhammad took the ball out of Bryce Young's hand on third-and-14 and Calijah Kancey ran it in: 17-0, worth -7.69 EPA and 10.9 points of win probability, and Carolina had not yet crossed midfield with the offense.

Carolina's one real answer came immediately — Young to Waller, 50 yards, deep middle, +4.49 EPA — and the extra point was blocked and returned for a defensive two. A 17-7 game became 19-6. From there it was collection: a Zyon McCollum interception at 2:39 of the second set up a nine-play drive ending in a 14-yard touchdown to Tez Johnson with :12 on the clock, 33-6 at the half. Tykee Smith picked Young again at Q3 9:03, Carolina failed 4th-and-1 from the Tampa 5 at Q3 1:55, and Sean Tucker's 46-yard touchdown off a 99-yard drive made it 43-6. Garbage time ran to 36 Carolina plays and 25 Tampa plays.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The engine blended Tampa to a 57.6% pass rate against a 57.3% season rate. Tampa played at 18.3%, and at 14.9% on early downs against a 45.9% expected rate. That single number explains Mayfield (-150.3 pass yards), Otton (zero catches on 88.9% of snaps), Hurst (zero), Godwin (27 yards on five targets) and the opposite-direction blowups for Irving, Gainwell and Tucker. Nothing in the projection was allowed to collapse that way, because the blend is a neutral-script number and the game was never neutral after 11:15 of the second quarter.

The efficiency read underneath it was sound and under-weighted: Carolina's run defense was carried in the inputs at 5.12 yards per carry and 2.96 yards before contact, the worst in the file, against a Tampa offense already at 4.76 and 2.71. Add a 42.2% blitz rate and a 6.61 average box faced, and the eight explosive runs were a forecastable outcome of light fronts behind pressure. Carolina's ten-point shortfall, by contrast, was mostly bad luck piled on a real problem: three giveaways from a quarterback with a 1.0% season interception rate, but under a 33.3% sack-or-hit rate with five sacks and Taylor Moton injured mid-game.

The plan against the game

The plan named the hinge and then bet against it. Its swing factor S4 — "whether Tampa's run game functions early" — was decisive, and its underdog path read almost verbatim: Tampa on schedule, Irving on the soft per-carry edge, Tampa winning time of possession. But it attached that path to Mayfield restoring an intermediate passing layer and projected a 20-17 grind, so six of its calls (Mayfield, Godwin, Otton, plus Young, McMillan, Tremble on the other side) were passing-volume bets in a game that produced 42 combined attempts. Its structural edges were right — Hubbard's under cashed at 33 yards, the Coker regression cashed twice, the Mayfield passing-TD under cashed for exactly the stated reason that Tampa scores in the red zone on the ground. The record was 4-8, pace came in slow rather than average, and both leans failed in opposite directions.

Player by player

Irving's 24-106-2 was role plus script: 58.7% snap share, three runs of 10+, both touchdowns from inside the five. Gainwell's 14 carries for 88 was the rotation taking over at 30.2% of snaps, with a 29-yard run at Q2 6:32; Tucker's 52 yards came on seven carries with two stuffs and a 46-yard touchdown, a negative-EPA line with a huge yardage total. Mayfield was efficient on his handful (+0.481 EPA per dropback, 1-for-1 deep, cpoe +1.6) at an aDOT of 2.6 — volume, not form. Young took 33 dropbacks for -0.612 EPA each, a -15.7 cpoe, and lost 23% of the snaps to Pickett. Waller's 50-yard touchdown on two targets at 44.6% snaps is a one-play line, not a role change; McMillan's six targets led the receivers but Coker owned the larger air-yards share, which complicates the "alpha" framing even before the 56-play ceiling.

The defenses

Charting for this game is snap counts only — no pressure or coverage rows — so read it through the box. Tampa, tagged "no pass rush" in four of six prior games, produced five sacks and six hits with Diaby (three TFL), Vea and Muhammad getting home at better than double its 16.9% season pressure rate. That is the least repeatable part of the result. Carolina, missing Phillips and Incoom, allowed zero sacks and one hit on 11 dropbacks against a line without Goedeke, and surrendered 5.3 yards per carry to both edges and 6.1 up the middle while blitzing at 46.2%. Its red-zone defense gave up three touchdowns on four trips, consistent with the 81.8% rate it carried in — and with Week 1 (Chicago 4-for-4) and Week 6 (Philadelphia 2-for-2).

What the model should take from this

Three things. First, the pass-rate blend needs a game-state conditional: a team with a run-game edge and a two-score lead does not regress to its season average, it goes to 15%. Second, Carolina's play volume is the load-bearing input for every pass-catcher on that roster and is controlled by the opponent — 56 plays against 68.4 expected. Third, separate the signal from the bounce: the pressure on Young and the run lane against Carolina's front repeat; the muffed punt, the blocked extra point returned for two, and two interceptions from a 1.0%-INT quarterback do not.

Totals

Team totals derived from the market can still be 20 points light when non-offensive points are in play. Tampa's 43 included a fumble-return TD and a defensive two off a blocked extra point — 8 points its offense never produced on only 64 passing yards.

Trigger: A team with a pressure edge facing a QB with elevated sack-or-hit exposure: the over has a scoring path that has nothing to do with yardage projections.

A quarterback's season INT rate of 1.0% does not insulate him from a three-turnover day when the pocket breaks; two picks and a strip-sack came with a 33.3% pressure rate.
