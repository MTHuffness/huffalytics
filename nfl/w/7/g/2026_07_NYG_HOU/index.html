The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

The Giants were a 10-point road dog with a first-time starter and they won outright in overtime by running the football 34 times for 188 yards and four rushing touchdowns — against a Houston front that had allowed 3.43 yards a carry, third-best in the league, entering the day. The projection had New York at 98.5 rushing yards and 16.2 points; it scored a 0.01 on the rush-yardage component and a D overall. The over (48 vs 42.5) came in without a shootout: 119 plays at average pace, zero turnovers, and five touchdowns on drives that finished.

How it unfolded

Houston's opening possession ended in a 58-yard Fairbairn field goal, which flattered a drive that gained 30 yards. New York answered with the only sequence that mattered early: a fourth-and-2 end-around to Mooney at Q1 7:39 (+2.84 EPA) kept the drive alive, and McCarthy found Nabers for 42 to the 2 two plays later. From there the Giants moved the ball all afternoon and left points on the field — a 47-yard strike to Fields on 2nd-and-17 at Q1 4:05 (+4.10 EPA, the game's biggest play by EPA) ended in a missed 56-yard field goal, and a 27-yard Nabers completion before the half ended in a missed 44.

Houston's half-saving drive was eleven plays for 68 yards and it lived on New York's defensive pass interference: 21 yards on Phillips at Q2 2:29, 11 on Washington at Q2 1:10 — 32 free yards that never appear as passing yards — before Stroud hit Harrison on 3rd-and-6 and Stover on the two-point try. 14-14.

The third quarter was scoreless and the fourth was decided by one drive: fourteen plays, 80 yards, 8:18 of clock, starting at Q3 2:13. It included Skattebo's 19-yard run on 3rd-and-1, Winston's fourth-and-1 sneak at Q4 11:37, a 14-yard Fields catch on 3rd-and-9, and Skattebo's 16-yard touchdown off right guard. Houston tied it at 0:57 on a nine-play drive that started at the NYG 48 after a 10-yard sack of McCarthy. In overtime Houston went three-and-out, punted, and the Giants needed three plays: Skattebo 42 up the middle, then Singletary 30 up the middle to end it.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The passing side of the projection was close to right — McCarthy 252 against 238.3, Houston's carries 25 against 25.9, Nabers' targets exactly 9. The entire miss sits in two places. First, New York's ground game: the engine blended a 58.2% pass rate and 3.74 yards a carry, and the Giants ran on 52.3% of snaps at 5.5 a carry with five explosive runs and +0.301 EPA per rush. The mechanism is in the charting: Houston blitzed 48.4% of New York's snaps from a 6.78-man average box, with both starting safeties ruled out. A light box plus a blitz is a running lane, and 100 of the 188 yards came on 13 middle carries. Second, Houston's efficiency: 17-of-28 at -9.1 CPOE, 2-of-6 deep, five throws with no recorded depth, 3-of-13 on third down and -0.127 EPA per play between the 20s. The attempts the model projected were basically there; the yards per throw were not.

The points gap (27 vs 16.2) is partly finishing and partly bounce. The Giants went 3-for-3 in the red zone with 0.83 EPA per play inside the 20, converted every fourth down they tried, and gave the ball away zero times after three-turnover games in weeks 4 and 6. Against that, they missed two field goals. That is the cleanest version of this offense, and it is not the modal version.

The plan against the game

The plan nailed Houston and inverted New York. Its Texans profile — run lean, Marks at 17-20 carries, Stroud in the high 20s to low 30s, yardage split four or five ways instead of concentrated on Collins — is almost a transcript of the box score, and the three calls built on it (Marks over 59.5, Stroud under 249.5, Collins under 69.5) all cashed. But every Giants assumption flowed from a double-digit deficit that never existed: the plan expected 35-plus attempts and a 0.70 pass rate, got 28 and 0.462, and the two calls that depended on chase volume (Likely receptions, Skattebo under) were the ones that died. Notably, the plan listed Pitre and Blankenship out as a swing factor and still treated Houston's run-defense rank as fixed — the one inference that would have unlocked the game.

Player by player

Skattebo is the headline: 17 carries, 124 yards, four 10-plus runs, 56% of snaps in his first appearance since week 5, against a projection of 11.5 for 42. Singletary's 41 yards are mostly one 30-yard walk-off and should not be read as a usage change. Fields was the schedule-breaker — 4-of-5 for 83 with 74 air yards, all of it on 2nd-and-17, 3rd-and-4 and 3rd-and-9. Nabers hit his projected target count exactly and beat the yardage on two downfield shots. Likely is the cautionary line: 86% of snaps, four targets, 12 yards, with Fidone taking the one big intermediate throw. Winston's four carries for four yards included a touchdown and a fourth-down conversion — a live goal-line vulture. On the Houston side, Marks got everything the plan promised and gained 3.5 a carry with -5.24 EPA; Collins finished with six yards on five targets in a room that just absorbed Dell's 43-yard return game.

The defenses

No pressure charting landed for this game, so the proxies: New York's front produced three sacks and six QB hits on 30 dropbacks (20%), its best pressure game of the season against a team that had allowed pressure on 12.6%. Houston's defense generated three sacks of its own on 30 dropbacks but needed a near-coin-flip blitz rate to do it, and the cost was the run. The Giants' secondary leaked the familiar things — a 43-yard Dell catch, a 25-yard Boutte catch, two DPIs on a scoring drive — but held Houston to 5.5 yards an attempt overall.

What the model should take from this

Three things. Defensive run-rate inputs should be conditioned on back-end personnel: a top-three YPC-allowed figure built with healthy safeties is not the same number when both are out and the defense blitzes 40-plus percent. Second, the pass-rate blend for a dog needs a lead branch — the engine gave New York 58.2% and it ran at 52.3% because it was ahead or tied for 93% of regulation, and that single input drove the rush-yards, carries and points misses together. Third, the offense-side trap: Houston met the softest run front on the card with the only healthy back getting 19 carries and gained 66 yards, because its own rush EPA has been negative in every game this season. Opponent weakness is only worth what the offense can execute against it.

Props

Before betting a 'soft run defense' matchup, check the running offense's own EPA/rush, not just the defense's YPC allowed. Houston faced the league's worst run front (5.05 YPC allowed) with its only back getting 19 carries and produced 66 yards and -5.24 rushing EPA — because its rush EPA had been negative in all six prior weeks.

Trigger: Opponent allows 4.8+ YPC but the offense's season rush EPA is below -0.10: fade the rush-yards over and the rushing-TD prop even at a high implied total.

Props

Double-digit-dog passing volume is a script bet, not a role bet. McCarthy cleared 199.5 yards with 28 attempts and a 46% pass rate because he led or was tied for all but 316 seconds; the same call fails if the yards per attempt isn't there.

Trigger: Dog QB over on yards where the engine's implied attempts come from a projected deficit — prefer cases where the ADOT/CPOE edge (here +8.0 CPOE, 9.9 ADOT vs a safety-depleted secondary) carries the number independent of volume.

Sides

A top-3 run defense missing both starting safeties and blitzing 40%+ is not a top-3 run defense. Houston charted a 6.78 box and 48.4% blitz and gave up 100 yards on 13 middle carries.

Trigger: Both starting safeties out on a team whose season blitz rate is above 35% — upgrade the opponent's interior run game and its team total, and discount the defense's season YPC-allowed rank.

Trigger: Under priced on expected giveaways (projection carried 1.7 combined INTs) when both offenses finish red-zone trips — NYG 3/3, HOU 2/2 here.
