The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

The Rams won 35-21 and covered -3.5 without throwing a single ball more than 20 yards past the line of scrimmage. Between the 20s this was a coin flip — LA -0.021 EPA/play on 52 snaps, Vegas +0.035 on 53 — and the entire game sat inside the 20s: Los Angeles went five-for-five on red-zone trips with +0.681 EPA per snap there, Las Vegas went one-for-five with three Daniel Gay field goals and -0.609. The total went over 49.5 on 56 points, but nine of Vegas's came after 28-6 and 44 of its 67 snaps were garbage time.

How it unfolded

Vegas drew first blood in field position and got nothing for it: a 39-yard pass interference on Josh Wallace (Q1 11:44, +2.34 EPA) was immediately given back by Cousins's intentional grounding and ended in a missed 51-yarder. That was the template. Los Angeles's first scoring drive survived a holding penalty that erased a Corum touchdown at Q1 7:10 and still finished with Stafford's 3-yard throw to Davis Allen. From there the Rams scored on four straight possessions — an 84-yard, 12-play drive bridging the quarters, Nacua's 11-yard touchdown at Q2 4:20, and Kyren Williams's 17-yard left-end run at Q2 1:25 that made it 28-3 in 37 seconds of drive time.

Vegas's three best chances to climb back all failed at the margin: a 4th-and-3 incomplete to Cody White at Q2 2:00 (-2.79), a 4th-and-2 incomplete from the LA 13 at Q3 8:43 (-3.78), and a 4th-and-5 completion to Nailor reversed on a Rams challenge at Q3 7:08 (-3.64). Even a gift — Williams's fumble at the LA 22 in Q3 — produced zero points. The Raiders' two touchdowns came off Tucker's 37-yard catch to open the fourth and a Stafford interception by Nakobe Dean 16 seconds later. The Rams answered with a 12-play, 6:38 touchdown march, and that was the game.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The D grade is almost entirely a volume failure, and the volume failure is the scoreboard. Los Angeles never ran a snap while trailing; leading, it threw on 40.4% of 57 plays and finished at 38 carries for 199 yards against a blended projection of 26.4 for 120. The engine blended a .583 pass rate off a .639 season rate that was itself manufactured in weeks 3-6, when LA trailed and threw on two-thirds to all of its snaps. On the other side, Vegas was blended to 27.4 carries and ran 17 at 2.8 a clip — its 3.03 season YPC and 1.40 yards before contact were already the worst in the file, and the deficit removed the run entirely (73.1% pass).

The points miss on LA (26.5 projected, 35 actual) is finishing, not movement: five trips, five touchdowns, zero field goals, on 17 snaps with a .706 success rate. The engine keeps its touchdown split on the league constant because red-zone rate barely persists, which is the correct default and was wrong here by roughly a touchdown and a half.

The plan against the game

The pre-game plan nailed the macro: run at the Vegas front, finish the trips, let Cousins move and kick. Its one structural miss was the LA lean — it called balanced and the Rams played run (44.1% pass against a .639 season rate) — and that miss cascaded into the player book, where the checkdown volume it assigned to Williams and the intermediate volume it assigned to Higbee simply never existed in a 29-attempt game. Corum's 17 carries and 44.4% snap share were the information the plan did not have.

Player by player

Corum was the game's biggest prop story: 17-93 with a touchdown and +6.78 rush EPA, plus a 43-yard touchdown run wiped by Avila's face mask at Q3 4:18. Williams got the carries and the goal line (16-99-TD) but only two targets. The checkdowns went to Colby Parkinson (6 targets, 5-48, all 48 after the catch on 3 air yards) and Davis Allen (5 targets, 4-20, two touchdowns) while Higbee drew three looks. Nacua played through the calf at 84.7% of snaps with 10 targets and 68 yards — the single best projection in the file.

For Vegas, Jeanty's line inverted completely: 8 carries for 15 yards with -3.48 rush EPA, 8-of-8 receiving for 61 and the touchdown. Tucker's 103 yards came on five of twelve targets and were concentrated in two plays (41 and 37). Bowers got the underneath volume (73 yards, 26 YAC) and no touchdown. Cousins threw 44-45 times for 295-308 with a clean pocket and -0.182 EPA per dropback, -7.9 CPOE, 2-of-8 deep.

The defenses

Charting for coverage and pressure has not landed, so read the proxies. LA rushed four and got nothing — two sacks and three hits on 50 dropbacks (6.1% pressure) — which is the third time this season its 'no pass rush' tag has fired. What it does have is a secondary that holds at the goal line: Vegas got 53 field snaps at +0.035 EPA and 14 red-zone snaps at -0.609, the same shape as ARI's 5 trips and 0 touchdowns in week 6. Vegas blitzed LA on 37.5% of snaps and generated 10% pressure; it also surrendered 5.2 to 6.7 yards per carry in every run direction against a 71% under-center, 76.8%-motion Rams offense.

What the model should take from this

Three things. First, the pass-rate blend needs a score-state correction for favorites whose season rate was built while trailing — that one input drove four of the five big team-level misses. Second, the Rams' backfield is a near-even committee, not a bell cow, and the short passing game runs through tight ends, not the back. Third, the penalties and turnovers that tag LA almost weekly are real — two touchdowns erased, two giveaways worth -10.3 EPA — and the team still won by 14, which is the useful lesson: those costs are survivable when the opponent cannot run and cannot finish.
