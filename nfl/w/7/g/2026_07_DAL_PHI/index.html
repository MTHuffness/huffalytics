The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

Dallas won 38-21, covered -4.5 and sent a 43.5 total over by fifteen and a half points, and it did so in the least expected way: by running the ball 38 times for 204 yards against a Philadelphia front that offered no resistance, while Prescott threw 27 times from a pocket that produced zero sacks and zero quarterback hits. The projection had Dallas at 24 points on 35.7 attempts and 105 rush yards. The Philadelphia side of the projection, meanwhile, was almost exact — 21 points, 211 pass yards, 121 rush yards against 19.5/218/111 — because a backup quarterback chasing all night at a 5.1 depth of target is a very predictable object.

How it unfolded

Aubrey's 55-yarder opened the scoring, Barkley answered with a 29-yard run and an 8-yard touchdown on a four-play, 70-yard drive, and the game briefly looked like the possession fight Philadelphia needed. It stopped looking like one at Q1 9:05, when Prescott hit Flournoy for 35 on 3rd-and-8 (+3.65 EPA) and Dallas finished an 82-yard drive with a Lamb touchdown. Philadelphia got one gift — the Q1 3:18 aborted snap that Quinnen Williams recovered and then handed back with a defensive offside — and converted it into the 14-10 lead on a 15-play drive. Dallas then reeled off a 14-play, 81-yard touchdown march (Pickens, 21 yards on 3rd-and-3) and a 78-yard, 1:14 two-minute field-goal drive for 20-14.

Philadelphia's best stretch came out of the half: 17 plays, 84 yards, 9:16 off the clock, a converted 4th-and-1 to Lemon for 15 (+3.05 EPA, the biggest WPA play of the night), and a 21-20 lead. Dallas needed ten plays to take it back — Lamb for 16 and a two-point run — and from there it was a slow bleed: a 61-yard field-goal drive opened by a 34-yard Javonte Williams run, Overshown's interception of a Goedert checkdown at Q4 7:55 (-5.37 EPA), a turnover on downs, and a six-play touchdown drive starting at the PHI 16.

Why the game diverged from the projection

Two things, both on the Dallas side. First, the lean: the engine blended Dallas to a 59.2% pass rate off a 67.5% season figure that was entirely chase-manufactured (71-73% in weeks 5-6). Given a lead for 2,442 of 3,600 seconds, Dallas threw on 41.8% of snaps, 35.7% while leading, 21.4% in the second half, and an early-down rate of 40.4% against a 50.9% expectation. Second, the efficiency: the blend shrank Dallas's 3.74 season ypc toward 4.07, and it ran 5.4, with 20 carries left for 137 (6.8) and five explosive runs. Add zero sacks on 28 dropbacks and a 13.0 adot producing 5-of-10 deep completions, and 0.376 field EPA per play is what 38 points looks like. The model's team score (0.615) is almost entirely that; the player score (0.788) survived because the pass-catcher shares were right.

The plan against the game

The plan went 9-for-11 on calls and got the shape right — Dallas ahead, Philadelphia throwing 35 times underneath without the efficiency to convert it, the tight end and the slot as the whole target tree, Cooper priced on explosives he never got. What it undersold was how far the lean would swing: it called Dallas "balanced" and got a run team, and it wrote 24-16 where the real number was 38-21. Its one structural miss was Pierce, and that traces to the unknown it had already flagged: Barkley came back on 15% of snaps, so the backup kept the early-down work.

Player by player

Javonte Williams, 25-155-1, is the headline and the lesson: no other back active above change-of-pace, a 4th-and-1 conversion, the 34-yard explosive, and the clincher. Lamb drew 12 targets and 176 air yards — a 51.6% air-yards share — but caught five for 74 with two touchdowns; the targets are the role, the yards are boom-bust at this depth. Flournoy outsnapped Pickens (77.9% to 60.3%) and produced 4-4-70. On the other side, Lemon was the only Philadelphia receiver who mattered (8 targets, 7-67-1, 37 YAC), Goedert's seven targets carried 25 total air yards and the interception, and Barkley's 62 yards came on seven carries at 8.9 a pop while Pierce handled 14.

The defenses

Philadelphia's is the one the market should care about: 0.0% pressure rate against Prescott, no sacks, no hits — the fourth 'no pass rush' game in six weeks — plus 204 rushing yards surrendered to the offense's left. Dallas's defense gave up 121 on 25 carries (4.84) and allowed 17- and 15-play drives, but it also won the only turnover and held Philadelphia to one explosive pass, which was less a scheme achievement than the consequence of Smith and Wicks being out. Charting isn't published for this game, so the pressure read is from sack/hit rates only.

The blueprint

To beat Philadelphia you need to hold the ball: average-or-better run efficiency and third downs, because the front will not make you throw it away. To beat Dallas you need explosives — four 'explosive passing' tags and three 'run game' tags in six weeks — and then you need to get the Cowboys behind, where they abandon the run and give it away. Philadelphia had half the formula (4.84 a carry) and none of the other half (0-for-2 deep). That's the trap: Dallas's defensive profile looks exploitable to everyone, but an offense at -0.102 season field EPA with no outside receivers cannot cash it.

What the model should take from this

The blended pass rate is too conservative for a team whose season rate is chase-built. When the model projects a favorite whose recent pass rates came while trailing, it should push the attempt count down and the lead back's carries up harder than a simple blend does — 27 attempts and 38 carries was not an outlier, it was the first clean observation of Dallas in a lead. And the Barkley line is the prop lesson: a soft-tissue return can be efficient without being voluminous, so project snap share first and yards second.
