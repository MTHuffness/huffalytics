The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

San Francisco out-gained Atlanta 411 to 295, ran more plays, converted 3 of 4 fourth downs, finished both red-zone trips, and lost by nine. Two numbers hold the whole game: four turnovers to zero, and zero San Francisco sacks or QB hits on 34 Atlanta dropbacks. Without Bosa the 49ers' front produced nothing, Penix played the cleanest game of his season (25/34, +0.256 EPA/dropback, three touchdowns), and every time San Francisco approached scoring range in the second half it gave the ball back. Atlanta's own inefficiency — 2 touchdowns on 5 red-zone trips, three Folk field goals — kept the total at 49 against a 49.5 line, so the projection's only correct market call was the one its red-zone read earned.

How it unfolded

San Francisco opened with a 10-play, 69-yard touchdown drive built on the 24-yard McCaffrey checkdown at Q1 11:52 and a 4th-and-1 McCaffrey plunge. Branch's 61-yard kickoff return gave Atlanta a short field for Folk's first three. Kaelon Black's 30-yard run on the first snap of the second quarter made it 14-3, and that was the peak. Atlanta answered with a 10-play, 68-yard touchdown drive, and then the hinge: Black fumbled at the Atlanta 29 at Q2 4:24 (-5.61 EPA, -0.15 WPA) and the Falcons bled 4:20 off the clock into a 33-yard field goal with two seconds left — 14-12 instead of 21-9.

The third quarter was all Atlanta ball control. A 10-play, 59-yard drive put them ahead. San Francisco reached the Atlanta 25 and Purdy was intercepted by Bates at the 10 (Q3 5:41). Atlanta then went 15 plays and 70 yards, kept alive by a 20-yard DPI on Jones at 3rd-and-8 from the Atlanta 46 (Q3 1:00) — twenty yards of drive that never show up in Penix's passing total — and finished with London's 21-yard score on the first snap of the fourth. Down 26-14, SF moved to the Atlanta 23 and Za'Darius Smith sacked Purdy on 4th-and-10, then cut it to 26-20 on a drive that lived on two Atlanta penalties (defensive holding, face mask). A Daniels interception returned to the SF 19 at Q4 2:42 set up Folk's third field goal and ended it.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The engine's volume was fine: SF 65 plays against 61.3 projected, ATL 63 against 60.9, Penix 213 yards against 218.4. Two inputs failed. First, the blended 7.1% sack rate on Atlanta's offense produced an expectation of 2.4 sacks taken; the actual was zero sacks, zero hits, a 0.0% pressure rate — a direct consequence of Bosa being Out, and consistent with the 'no pass rush' tags SF already carried in weeks 3 and 4. That turned a projected 1.3 passing touchdowns into three. Second, the 0.7-interception projections on both sides met four San Francisco turnovers worth -17.81 EPA, every one in Atlanta territory or at midfield in a one-score game. San Francisco's rush total actually beat the projection (123 on 23 carries) and its passing yardage beat it by 46; the points came up six short entirely through access to the red zone, not efficiency inside it.

The plan against the game

The plan called the Atlanta side almost line for line — 34 attempts, Bijan at 20 carries for 63 yards, London at 10 targets without a chunk game, Pitts held to 22 — and both of those downgrades cashed, along with the Kittle and McCaffrey funnel calls. The leg it built the script on, pressure on Penix, inverted completely, and that single failure dragged the Penix touchdown under and the Purdy interception under with it. The Dotson upgrade is the most instructive miss: the mechanism showed up (three targets, 57 air yards, two deep shots), but the yardage became an illegal contact and a 20-yard DPI instead of receptions. Checks confirm the drift from the written script: pace fast (128 plays against 121.5), San Francisco leaning pass at 0.646 against a 0.510 season rate, Atlanta balanced at 0.540 rather than pass-leaning.

Player by player

Kittle was the offense: 10 targets, 8-88, 90.9% of snaps, two drive-extending conversions, and the late punch-out by Bates. Watkins is the number to carry: a 1.0-target projection turned into 6 targets and a team-high 95 air yards on 75.8% of snaps while the receiver room is thin. McCaffrey's receiving work matched (6-61 on 7 targets) but his 51.5% snap share and 7 carries against 13.4 projected are a committee signal, with Black taking 12 carries — 63 yards that were one 30-yard run plus two stuffs and -4.57 rushing EPA. On Atlanta's side, Bijan's split is the model lesson: 20 carries at 3.15 ypc with -5.27 rushing EPA, offset by 5-5 for 59 receiving yards on one air yard. London's 43.9% air-yards share is bankable; his yardage is not. Pitts is a touchdown-or-nothing line at 5 short targets.

The defenses

San Francisco's front was invisible: 4.14 average rushers and a 30.6% blitz rate against Atlanta per charting, and not one hit. The run defense did its job — Atlanta's middle runs went 9 for 11 yards, rush success 0.370 — but allowing 25 completions at a 6.1 aDOT with 129 YAC is what a defense without a pass rush looks like. Atlanta's defense was the opposite: two sacks, three takeaways in coverage from Bates and Daniels, and then 2-for-2 surrendered in the red zone, which is its recurring hole (weeks 4, 5, 6). Both DPI/illegal-contact flags came against SF corners on deep balls, a facet worth flagging rather than calling a trait.

What the model should take from this

Two things repeat and one does not. The repeating ones: SF's pass rush collapses without Bosa, and Atlanta's quarterback is a different player depending on whether the opponent can get there with four. Price both. The non-repeating one is the turnover column — a forced fumble, a punch-out and two chase-mode picks are not a San Francisco trait, and the team-level inputs (0.012 INT rate, an Atlanta defense generating 0.4%) said so. The right-and-wrong lesson of the day is the red zone: the dossier's finishing split correctly produced the Under in a 49-point game, and correctly produced the wrong side.
