The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

The Chargers out-threw the Rams by a hundred yards and lost by fifteen. Los Angeles-the-Rams covered the 6.5 and the game went 13.5 points under a 46.5 total, and both results come from the same place: LAC reached the red zone once, scored zero touchdowns, and gave Herbert's two interceptions back as a short-field field goal and a two-play touchdown drive. The Rams ran 27 times for 129 yards, finished two of three trips inside the 20, and asked Stafford to throw 25 times.

How it unfolded

The first quarter was a trade of giveaways and punts. Stafford's 3rd-and-6 throw to Mumpfield was picked by Still at Q1 12:12 (-4.14 EPA); the Chargers gave it right back when Lake intercepted a short right throw intended for Kolar at Q1 7:22 and returned it 45 yards to the LAC 19 — -7.07 EPA, -0.211 WPA, the largest single swing of the day. McManus's 30-yarder followed. Dicker answered from 34, then 46, then 41, and at the two-minute warning the Chargers led 9-3 having driven 51, 47 and 57 yards without a touchdown.

The half turned on 1:36 of Rams offense. A 28-yard kickoff return to the LA 34, a no-huddle 40-yard shot to Atwell at Q2 1:29, Nacua for 13 to the LAC 1 at 0:44, Williams in from the 1 at 0:22: 10-9. Herbert's 54-yard answer to McConkey reached the LA 1 with four seconds left and produced nothing.

From there it was the Rams' game to manage. Eight plays, 79 yards to open the third, Corum 23 up the middle for the touchdown at 10:48. McDuffie intercepted Herbert at Q3 4:37 and returned it to the LAC 22; Nacua scored from 18 forty-nine seconds later. 24-9, win probability 1.0, and 33 garbage-time snaps between the two teams.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The volumes were nearly right and the efficiency distribution was not. LAC's 32 attempts and 25 carries landed within three of projection; their 20 projected points became 9 because the drives died in two places the model's point estimate doesn't see — the turnover column and the 20-yard line. Their field EPA was -0.353 and red-zone EPA -0.986, which means even the yardage was low-leverage: 6-of-17 on third down, 13.6% rush success, 2.6 yards a carry with runs left going 7 for 4.

On the Rams' side the miss is entirely script. The engine blended a 0.556 pass rate from a season mark of 0.611 that was manufactured in weeks 3 and 5 while chasing. Leading for 2,156 of the game's seconds, LA threw on 32.4% of snaps, and Stafford finished 8 attempts and 81 yards light. The rushing row (129 vs 120.4) and the points row (24 vs 26.5) were fine; the passing rows absorbed all the error.

The plan against the game

The pregame plan called this almost exactly: Rams lead, hand off into the softest front on the field, finish inside the 20 while Herbert throws from a clean pocket into field goals and takeaways. Seven of ten calls hit, LA's run lean held (49.1% pass against a 61.1% season rate), and the Stafford under, the Herbert interception over and the Herbert pass-TD under all came in for the reasons given. Where it slipped was in the margins: pace ran slow (112 plays against a 121.5 league pair) so Stafford never reached the low-30s attempts the plan wanted, LAC's forced-passing never pushed past balanced, and two downgrades built on "his role shrinks with the room healthy" — Gadsden and Johnston — were simply wrong about a receiving corps that spreads 32 attempts across six names at a 5.1 aDOT.

Player by player

McConkey is the headline: 90.2% of snaps after three weeks of single-digit shares, 6 targets, 86 yards, a 37.7% air-yards share and both long plays. That is a role change the model has to re-rate, not a variance pop. Gadsden went 5-for-5 for 52 on 47.5% of snaps; Johnston 4-for-4 for 37 at 82%. Kolar's line — 4 targets, 1 catch, two of the remaining three intercepted, -13.28 EPA — is the cautionary one: the target volume was projected to the decimal and the outcome was unrepeatable.

For the Rams, Nacua took 12 of 25 attempts for 11 catches at 96.3% of snaps, with 65 of his 80 yards after the catch. Williams kept the goal-line carry and lost the receiving role (1 target, 3 yards) the moment the lead arrived, which is how a projected 89.5 rush+rec became 59. Corum's 11 for 73 with two explosive runs at a 31.5% snap share is now a two-week pattern and the live threat to Williams's ceiling. Stafford: 4.2 aDOT, -9.0 CPOE, one deep attempt all day.

The defenses

Neither pass rush showed up — 2 sacks and 4 hits on 36 Herbert dropbacks, 1 sack and 2 hits on Stafford's 26. Coverage charting hasn't landed, so read it through the proxies: LAC faced a 25.9% blitz rate and still completed 75% at a 5.1 aDOT; the Rams won on the two throws that mattered, both breaking on short in-breakers to a tight end. The Rams' front, by contrast, decided the run: nine of Corum's eleven carries went up the middle, LA averaged 4.9 there, and the Chargers managed 0.6 a carry running left.

The Rams' defense is bend-don't-break and has been for three weeks: 0, 1 and now 0 red-zone touchdowns allowed in its last three games while conceding field yardage at or above league rates.

What the model should take from this

Three things. First, the engine's blended pass rate is contaminated for teams whose attempt totals come from deficits; use the leading-state rate when the team is laid 6.5 or more. Second, LAC's 20-point market-derived team total was never consistent with a -0.295 season field EPA and a five-week turnover tag — when the pre-game inputs say a team moves it badly in low leverage and gives it away, the team total should be shaded below the market implication even when the spread is honest. Third, snap share leads target share: McConkey's return to a full route tree was visible on the injury report and cost the projection 67 yards on one line.
