The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

Chicago never trailed, covered +3 as a road underdog and dragged a 46.5 total over the number almost single-handedly. The game was not decided between the 20s — the Bears' field EPA was a modest +0.063 per play — it was decided at the goal line and on third down. Chicago took four red-zone trips and scored four touchdowns at +0.582 EPA per snap; Seattle took four and came away with one touchdown, one field goal, a blocked 33-yarder and a -0.419 red-zone EPA, while converting one third down in thirteen attempts. That is a 17-point game out of a roughly even yardage night.

How it unfolded

The opening possession set the terms. Seattle went three-and-out, and the Bears answered with 17 plays, 80 yards and 8:32, surviving 4th & 1 at midfield on Swift's five-yard run (EPA +2.54) before Burden's one-yard touchdown. Chicago then added field goals of 44 and 38 off drives built on Burden's 23-yard dig and the 24-yard Odunze throw out of the Swift direct-snap look. Seattle's only real counterpunch was a 43-yard Smith-Njigba touchdown on 3rd & 10 with 37 seconds left in the half — the single biggest play of the night at +5.37 EPA and +.148 WPA — which made a 13-3 hole a 13-9 one.

The second half was a procession of Seattle possessions that produced yards and nothing else: a 12-play, 49-yard drive ending in a blocked field goal; a three-and-out with Sweat's eight-yard sack; a 12-play, 59-yard drive that died on fourth-and-four. The one Seattle break, Lawrence's strip of Loveland at the CHI 21, produced a short-field touchdown and a two-point conversion to make it 20-17 with 11:51 left. Chicago's response took six plays and 61 yards, the chunk a 39-yard Raymond end around on 3rd & 9 immediately after a delay-of-game flag. Darnold's interception by Lynch at his own 22 four minutes later ended it.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The engine had the volume almost exactly right — 34 Chicago attempts against 32 projected, 126 combined plays against a 121.5 league pair, Smith-Njigba's 11 targets on the nose — and missed the scoring because it declines, by design, to apply red-zone touchdown rates. The inputs plainly said Seattle allowed touchdowns on 81.8% of trips with the league's worst red-zone EPA allowed; the projection still split Chicago's trips into 1.3 rushing touchdowns and 1.6 field goals. Chicago kicked zero inside the 20. That is the whole 12.2-point team miss, and it is a mixed verdict: red-zone rates are the least persistent thing in the dossier, but .818 across seven weeks with 7-for-7 in weeks 4-6 is more than a small-sample wobble.

Seattle's misses were cleaner. The 19 carries against 25.3 projected are pure script — 59 of 62 snaps trailing, pass rate 66.1% against a 53.8% season rate. The 188 passing yards against 236.7 are not: cpoe -16.9, 17-of-34, six attempts at an unknown depth all incomplete, 22.5% pressure behind a line without Cross. That is an efficiency signal.

The plan against the game

The plan's underdog paragraph is close to a transcript — the quick game survived the rush, Loveland and the backs moved the chains, the red-zone trips came back as sevens instead of Santos threes, and Darnold gave one away. What the plan could not see was Seattle's offense being this bad from behind, so it wrote 24-23 instead of 34-17. Its three losing calls all share one root: it read Seattle's league-leading passing yards allowed as coverage quality when a meaningful part of it was allowing 49.9 snaps per game. Give that defense 69 snaps and Williams clears 224.5, Odunze takes 145 air yards, and Burden clears an under written on the premise that he needed an explosive he never got.

Player by player

Swift is the instructive line: 27 carries for 69 yards, 2.56 per carry, four stuffs, zero runs of ten-plus — the efficiency read on Seattle's front was right — and three touchdowns from inside the five. The under lost on volume the lead created; the touchdown over won on the matchup the inputs advertised. Loveland (9 targets, 7-65, 92.9% snaps) and Burden (9 targets, 7-56 on 57% snaps) are role facts worth keeping with Kmet out. Odunze is the one to watch for props: six targets, 145 air yards, 45% of the team's air yards, three of Chicago's four 20-plus pass plays. On the other side, Smith-Njigba's 11 targets and 56.6% air-yards share are durable; his 118 yards rode two plays. Kupp at 92% snaps and three targets is a touchdown-or-nothing line in this tree, and the Seattle backfield — Price 10 carries, Charbonnet 6 on no projection at all — never had enough volume to teach us the split.

The defenses

No participation charting here, so this is pbp proxies. Chicago generated three sacks and six hits on 40 dropbacks (15.4%) with contributions from Simmons, Sweat, Bush and Booker on the pressure that forced the interception — real, but below the 19%-plus rates that have actually broken Chicago's own offense this year. Seattle's front did its job between the 20s (3.6 yards per carry allowed, nothing before contact) and then surrendered the goal line four times. Chicago's secondary gave up only two explosive passes, but one was the 43-yard touchdown, consistent with a season of conceding chunks while taking the ball away. Chicago's defensive flags gave Seattle 61 drawn yards, including a holding at Q4 12:42 that erased a Lewis interception on the drive that became Kupp's touchdown — free yards that never show in the passing line.

What the model should take from this

Two things. First, the red-zone split is being left on the table: when an opponent's allowed trip-to-touchdown rate is near .800 over seven weeks, that belongs in the team total and the side, not just in an anytime-touchdown note. Second, yards-allowed leaders need a snap-count adjustment before their matchup inputs are trusted — Seattle's 5.19 yards per attempt allowed and 158 passing yards per game were partly a function of facing 49.9 plays, and a 71-play opponent that leads wire-to-wire dissolves both. Everything else here — the even turnover count, the gadget 39-yarder, the blocked kick — is bounce, and the model should not move on any of it.

Traps · where a line can fool you next time
Totals

When the engine reports an opponent red-zone TD rate allowed near .800 but applies the league constant to the touchdown split, the favorable offense's team total and anytime-TD prices are stale. Seattle's .818 was 7 weeks of data; Chicago went 4-for-4 and beat its projected points by 12.2.

Trigger: opp_defense red-zone TD rate allowed > .75 with red-zone EPA allowed > +0.3, paired with an offense at or above league red-zone rate.

Props

'Fewest passing yards allowed' is sometimes a volume statistic. Seattle allowed 49.9 plays per game entering; once Chicago ran 69 snaps and held the ball 35 minutes, the per-game yardage suppression evaporated and Williams went for 247 against a 224.5 line.

Trigger: A yards-allowed-leading defense facing a top-5 plays-per-game offense (CHI 71.4) that is likely to lead — check opponent snaps allowed before taking the QB under.

Props

Pairing a RB rushing-yards under with his anytime-TD over is only coherent if the game script is neutral. Swift hit 2.56 yards per carry exactly as the efficiency read said and still cleared 59.5 because the lead bought him 27 carries and three goal-line scores.

Trigger: Underdog RB whose team is a live candidate to lead wire-to-wire, with the other back ruled out — price the carry distribution off win probability, not off the season average.

Props

Seattle's rushing props are a lead-dependent bet. Trailing for 59 of 62 snaps produced 19 carries split three ways (Price 10, Charbonnet 6, Holani 1) and 81 yards; the plan's single bettable back (Wilson) didn't even take a snap.

Trigger: SEA as an underdog or with a backfield in flux — fade team rush attempts and avoid any single-back rushing over without confirmed roles.

Props

Quarterback unders on a heavy-volume offense that is not trailing can still lose on explosive efficiency. Chicago took only 34 attempts but produced 4 passes of 20+ (Odunze 145 air yards) against a defense that had gone whole games without allowing one.

Trigger: Opposing secondary at 8.5+ yards per attempt allowed on the season even when its raw yardage rank is elite — the YPA row, not the yards row, prices the explosive risk.
