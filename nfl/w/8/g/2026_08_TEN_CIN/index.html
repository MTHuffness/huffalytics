The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

A 13.5-point dog projected for 16 points scored 36, led for 1,942 of the game's seconds, and lost 43-36 when Joe Burrow walked into the end zone from nine yards out in overtime. The total was 45.5; the teams played to 79 on 140 snaps. The projection's two market calls that pointed the same way — Cincinnati to win, Tennessee +13.5 — both cashed, and everything else burned: an F grade (0.398), with Burrow's 462 passing yards against a 245.4 projection as the largest single error in the file.

How it unfolded

Tennessee started as the aggressor: a 46-yard Slye field goal, then Ward's 4th-and-1 scramble at Q1 12:46 (+2.73 EPA) and, two drives later, Pollard's 40-yard touchdown off right tackle at Q1 8:04 for 10-0. Hooker's first interception of Burrow at Q1 6:56 made it 13-0.

Cincinnati's answer came through the air and only through the air. A Burrow-to-Brown 20-yard touchdown was wiped by offensive holding at Q2 13:34; on the replayed 3rd-and-18 he threw 30 yards to Chase for the score anyway (+4.70 EPA). Tennessee gave one back on Robinson's fumble at the CIN 39, Cincinnati got nothing from it (Martin's 15-yard sack on 4th-and-2 at Q2 3:12), then took the lead on Endries' one-yard score with 23 seconds left in the half.

The third quarter was a turnover exchange. Hooker picked Burrow again at the TEN 35; Mafe stripped Ward on the very next drive and Lawrence recovered at the TEN 29, the game's biggest EPA swing (-6.32), and Higgins scored on the next snap. Tennessee answered with an 11-play drive that lived on two Cincinnati DPIs and tied it with a two-point conversion. Hooker's third pick at the TEN 5 gave Tennessee the ball on its own goal line, and Pollard was tackled in the end zone for a safety at Q3 0:50.

From there it was four straight touchdown drives: Taylor's forced fumble on Brown at Q4 13:40 (-0.268 WP, the largest swing of regulation) set Tennessee up at the CIN 48 for the Dike score; Gesicki's 25-yarder put Cincinnati back ahead; Robinson's 15-yard touchdown plus a two-point conversion made it 36-29; and Burrow drove 81 yards in 2:29 to tie it with 1:20 left. Lawrence's 13-yard sack at Q4 0:32 killed Tennessee's last regulation chance. In overtime Burrow found Higgins for 21 on 2nd-and-1 and ran it in.

Why the game diverged from the projection

One assumption carried the whole error. The engine blended Cincinnati to a 0.567 pass rate and 26.9 carries, which is what a 13.5-point favorite does with a lead. Cincinnati ran 50 of its 73 snaps while trailing, threw at .726 (.811 in the second half) and handed off 17 times for 75 yards at -0.469 EPA per rush. That alone explains +14.8 attempts and +216.6 passing yards. Tennessee's miss is different: the volume read was close (28 carries against 25, 40 attempts against 31.7) but the efficiency read collapsed — a team carrying -0.134 field EPA and 5.67 yards per attempt posted +0.087 field EPA, 5.6 yards a carry and five explosive passes. Add four Cincinnati giveaways and the possession math, and 16 projected points became 36.

The plan against the game

The plan identified the right ingredients and bet them all on one state. Higgins as the air-yards hog, Pollard as the entire backfield against a front that sells chunk runs, and Cincinnati scoring through the air rather than on the ground all came in — U1, U2, U3 and U6 are four clean mechanism hits. But five more calls (Brown's carries, Burrow's yardage, Chase's catch count, both interception props) were the same bet wearing different jerseys: that Cincinnati would be two scores up by the third quarter. Its largest lead was eight points and it spent nine snaps in a leading state. That is a read to fix, not a bounce — a team whose rush EPA is negative every week has no lead script to fall back on.

Player by player

Burrow's 38-of-48 for 462 and five touchdowns was efficiency (16.9 CPOE, 9-of-14 deep for 202) multiplied by deficit. Higgins at 12 targets and 121 air yards produced 155 yards and two scores in four chunk catches; Chase soaked 16 targets and 48.9% of the air yards and was the intended man on two of the three picks. Gesicki's 76 yards were two plays. On the other side, Pollard's usage was the projection's best work — 21 carries against 20.3, 3 catches for 20 against 2.9 for 19.5 — and his 117 yards came in two runs. The real learnable is Tennessee's target tree without Tate and with Ridley at 51.9% of snaps: Helm drew 10 targets for 76 against a 3.8 projection and Dike 6 for 71 and a touchdown against 1.4, with Dike leading the team in air-yards share on an 81.8% snap share. Ward's 304 came on volume and 136 team YAC with a -1.0 CPOE.

The defenses

Neither pass rush existed in bulk — 15.2% and 17.0% pressure, both near league — but both concentrated. Jacob Martin had three sacks on a 61% snap share, including the 15-yarder that ended Cincinnati's 4th-and-2 at the TEN 20. Mafe and Lawrence produced the two plays that cost Tennessee the game. Coverage charting has not landed for this game, so read the rest from proxies: Cincinnati's 9.4 aDOT and nine deep completions against a secondary that has allowed four-plus explosives in four weeks, and Tennessee's 7.4 aDOT short game that still produced five 20-plus plays. Red-zone behavior was the inversion of every pre-game input: Cincinnati 5 touchdowns on 6 trips, Tennessee 3 on 4, no field goals after Q1.

The blueprint

Tennessee gets beaten the same way every week — explosive passing over a secondary with no rush in front of it, and a red-zone 'strength' that is a sample artifact. A quarterback who can win at 9+ aDOT does not need anything else. Cincinnati gets beaten by the run and by its own inability to run: a back who hits 5+ yards a carry with chunk runs, combined with an offense that converts those drives into touchdowns rather than kicks, since the Bengals' defense surrendered zero field goals here. Tennessee ran that blueprint perfectly and still lost by seven, which is the warning — the executor was playing two standard deviations above its season.

What the model should take from this

Raise Cincinnati's pass-rate floor toward its season .629 regardless of the spread and stop treating the hand-off script as a given for a team with negative rush EPA. Treat the three interceptions and the zero as variance in both directions. And carry the target-reallocation read: when a WR1 is out and the WR2 is at half snaps, the vacated work goes to the tight end and the third receiver, and the model's 1.4-target projection on a man playing 81.8% of snaps is a usage error, not a projection miss.

Traps · where a line can fool you next time
Props

Do not price a double-digit favorite's pass rate off the lead it is supposed to have. CIN's engine blend was a 0.567 pass rate and 26.9 carries; it threw at .726 and ran 17 times because it trailed for 50 of 73 plays. When a favorite's rush EPA is deeply negative (-0.469 here, negative in five of six prior weeks), it has no run script to fall back on even when ahead.

Trigger: Favorite of 10+ whose season rush EPA/play is below -0.10: fade the 'lead shortens his day' QB unders and the RB rushing overs.

Totals

A team flagged for 'red zone defense' three weeks in a row is still a small sample. TEN entered allowing a 29.4% RZ TD rate and -0.538 RZ EPA and surrendered 5 TDs on 6 trips. Red-zone rates are the least persistent number in the file; do not shade a total down for them.

Trigger: Any total shaded under on a sub-35% red-zone TD rate allowed built on fewer than 20 trips.

Props

Target reallocation after a WR1 absence is a real, bettable signal; quarterback efficiency is not. With Tate out and Ridley at a 51.9% snap share, Helm drew 10 targets (projected 3.8) and Dike 6 (projected 1.4) for 147 combined yards — while Ward's CPOE was -1.0.

Trigger: WR1 out plus a questionable WR2: buy the TE and the No. 3 receiver's receptions/yards, not the QB's efficiency.

Props

Interception props are attempts-and-variance, not matchup. Burrow entered at a 1.4% INT rate against a defense that had forced takeaways in three of seven weeks and threw three picks on 48 attempts; Ward entered having given one away in five straight and threw 40 without one. Both sides of the same read lost.

Trigger: Any INT prop where the mechanism is 'script' rather than attempts — require a 45+ attempt projection before leaning over.
