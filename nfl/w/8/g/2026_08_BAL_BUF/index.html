The full story · the report's account, start to finish

The short version

Buffalo 36, Baltimore 10: the Bills covered -3 easily and the game went nine points under a 54.5 total, which is an unusual combination and tells you where it came from. Baltimore scored ten points on 50 snaps and 167 yards, with its only touchdown set up by a Josh Allen interception that gave them the ball on the BUF 17. The projection had the Ravens at 25.8 points and 248.5 passing yards; they threw for 91. Lamar Jackson took one dropback all afternoon. Everything else is downstream of that.

How it unfolded

Buffalo's opening possession was the template: ten plays, 65 yards, a 26-yard Shakir catch to flip field position, and Allen keeping it from the one on fourth-and-one. The Bills converted four of six fourth downs on the day and held the ball for 37:36. Baltimore's answer was three-and-out, three-and-out, and then a gift — Washington's interception at the BUF 17, Henry in from the 2 two plays later.

The second quarter settled it without ever looking decisive. Huntley was strip-sacked by Bishop at the BAL 32 (Q2 14:10, EPA -5.15), which became a Bass field goal; Fagnano was then intercepted by Igbinosun at midfield with 1:21 left (WPA -0.157), which became a 51-yarder and a 16-7 halftime lead instead of 13-7. Baltimore had already missed a 34-yarder after a Landon Jackson sack pushed them out of range.

Baltimore's one real drive opened the third quarter — 47 yards, fueled by a 22-yard Bateman catch plus 15 for roughing the passer — and died on penalties: Flowers false start at the BUF 5, then Stanley's holding erasing a 10-yard Henry touchdown. Field goal, 16-10. Buffalo answered with 77 yards in six plays, the 49-yard Allen-to-Moore throw doing all of it, and Cook finished from the 5. From there it was 40- and 23-yard Kincaid receptions in a game already decided.

Why the game diverged from the projection

One mechanism, loudly: Baltimore's quarterback. Jackson logged a single dropback; Huntley took five at -1.862 EPA per dropback with an 80% pressure rate before Fagnano took the other 25 at 48% pressure, 9 QB hits, aDOT 4.3 and CPOE -16.4. The engine blended 8.39 yards per attempt and a 70.3% completion rate — Jackson's numbers — into a 248.5-yard projection. The actual offense threw 24 of 27 attempts short for 69 yards.

The secondary mechanism is pressure, and it was forecastable. Baltimore took 4 sacks and 12 hits on 31 dropbacks, a 38.7% rate against the 14.6% their offense had allowed all season; they were tagged for pressure allowed in weeks 5 and 7. Buffalo blitzed on 42.5% of snaps and still got there with four.

On the Buffalo side the only big team-level miss was carries — 41 against 27.1 projected — which is pure script. Sixty-nine of their 79 snaps came with the lead, early-down pass rate 0.431 against an 0.487 expectation, and twenty of those plays were garbage time. That is noise, not a read to carry forward.

The plan against the game

The plan got Buffalo's shape right and its personnel wrong in both directions. Cook at 21 carries with the goal-line score, Allen's 34 attempts and his over-249.5 yards, Ty Johnson's checkdown yardage, Henry's capped rushing total, Andrews' fade — all of that came in, and the pre-snap-penalty swing factor cashed literally, costing Baltimore four points at the BUF 5. But the plan's spine was Jackson's return, and he did not play, which voided the entire underdog path and the three calls built on him (two missed, one hit for a reason the plan never gave). The learnable error is elsewhere: it bet Coleman as the air-yards alpha and faded Kincaid as a short-area target, and Buffalo's downfield offense ran through Moore and Kincaid for two touchdowns.

Player by player

Fagnano: 10-of-21, 91 yards, a pick, a fourth-quarter aborted-snap fumble. Flowers drew five targets and 36% of the air yards and caught none of them — role intact, quarterback not. Justice Hill was the beneficiary, 8 targets (2.8 projected) for 20 yards on -7 air yards, the classic pressured-backup distribution. Henry was actually fine: 11 carries, 4.8 a pop, +0.88 EPA, a touchdown scored and one erased.

For Buffalo, Kincaid (6-4-86-TD on 83 air yards) and Moore (7-4-82-TD on 103) were the game, while Coleman played 77% of snaps for four yards on three targets. Shakir's 5-64 on 13 air yards and 56 YAC is the stable floor in that room. Ray Davis's 30 yards on 6 carries is a blowout artifact and nothing more.

The defenses

Charting is snap counts only this week — no pressure or coverage attribution — so read it from proxies. Buffalo generated its 12 hits without heavy blitzing (Baltimore faced 4.72 rushers on average, a 62.5% blitz rate on a small denominator of dropbacks) and conceded one explosive pass all day. Baltimore's defense was the opposite: 4 explosive passes allowed, 3 red-zone touchdowns on 4 trips, +0.171 EPA per play inside the 20. The 49-yarder to Moore and the 40-yarder to Kincaid were coverage losses with Humphrey and Wallace in trail. Their front held up — Buffalo's 41 carries went for 3.6 a pop, -0.056 rush EPA — which is consistent with the pre-game input of 3.84 yards per carry allowed.

What the model should take from this

First, a quarterback-contingent projection is not a projection. The entire Baltimore team total, the Flowers line, the Henry touchdown price and the side all moved on one inactive. Build the branch.

Second, soft-secondary matchups require an offense that can reach them. Buffalo had allowed four or five explosive passes in six consecutive weeks and allowed one, because the opponent's team aDOT was 4.6. Pair the defensive weakness with the attacking offense's depth and pressure-allowed rate before you buy it.

Third, Baltimore's pre-snap flags remain the most persistent negative in their file — five of seven weeks, 2.86 per game — and they cost a touchdown again. Shade red-zone scoring props, not yardage.
