The read

Buffalo is the better team by a distance between the 20s (0.224 field EPA per play, 9.05 yards an attempt, 5.37 a carry) and is laid 5.5 in a dome. The way it scores is on the ground: every touchdown in Week 3 was a one- to three-yard run, Allen has a rushing score in all five games, and Cook has four rushing touchdowns in the last four weeks. Las Vegas gives up 5.21 yards a carry (31st) and 140.6 rushing yards a game (28th), and allows a touchdown on 80% of red-zone trips (30th). That is the Bills' half.

Las Vegas is not a team that gets shut out of this. Buffalo's defense has allowed 295.8 passing yards a game (31st), four or five explosive passes every single week, and a touchdown on 83.3% of red-zone trips against it — the worst finishing-defense number on the board. Cousins has thrown three touchdowns in three of five starts; Bowers has gone 116, 86 and 110 receiving yards in three games since returning and practiced in full Thursday (FantasyPros, Thu).

Why

The mechanism that decides volume is possession. Las Vegas runs 64.6 plays a game and cannot run the ball at all (2.99 a carry, 1.40 before contact against a 2.54 league mark), so once it trails, Cousins is throwing — 52 attempts the last time that happened, against Kansas City. Buffalo's run lean, by contrast, is conditional: our Week 4 review of the New England loss found the Bills threw on 60.3% of snaps because they never led. Favored here, the lean returns, which caps Allen's attempts and hands Cook and Allen the short-yardage scores.

The Raiders' counter-lever is pressure — second in the league at 22.8% — which pushes Allen out of the pocket. That raises his scramble yards more than it lowers them.

What would change it

DJ Moore and Josh Palmer are both questionable (Rotowire, Fri); Moore practiced in a non-contact jersey. If Buffalo falls behind, Allen's passing lines flip and Cook's carries evaporate — the exact break that cost us in Week 4. And if Buffalo blows it open, Cousins may not finish, as in Week 5.

The short version

Buffalo 33, Las Vegas 30, and both market calls that mattered lost: the Bills won outright but gave back the 5.5, and 63 points cleared a 53.5 total by nearly ten. The projection had the shape of this game almost right — Buffalo's entire team line is within a few points and a few attempts — and still graded a C, because the Raiders threw 41 times instead of 34.8, for 313 yards instead of 250.5, and the scoreboard ran hot on both ends. The thing that decided it was a single possession's worth of EPA: Cousins' third-quarter interception, Buffalo's immediate touchdown off it, and the three-point cushion that survived a 44-yard Tucker touchdown with 1:39 left.

How it unfolded

The first half was pure trade. Buffalo opened with a 30-yard, eight-play field-goal drive built on Allen's 14-yard third-and-8 throw to Ty Johnson (Q1 12:49, +2.77 EPA); Las Vegas answered with a 64-yard march keyed by a 26-yard Tucker catch on third-and-7 (Q1 8:50) and Jeanty's one-yard plunge. Buffalo's 90-yard touchdown drive in the second quarter — Coleman 21, Cook 23, Shakir nine for the score at 7:17 — was the game's longest sustained stretch of offense, and Las Vegas answered it with two field goals, the second built on a 14-yard defensive holding flag on Igbinosun at :19 that tied it 13-13 at the break.

The third quarter is where the margin was made and then partly given back. Buffalo got a 47-yard Bass field goal only because its own holding penalty at 9:57 erased an 18-yard Kincaid touchdown. Then Cousins threw into traffic for Mayer at 9:00 and Dorian Williams picked it off at the LV 32 — a -5.03 EPA play — and Allen hit Coleman from 12 on third-and-8 to go up 23-13. Las Vegas answered with a 16-play, 75-yard, 7:50 possession that produced three points, which is the Raiders' season in one drive.

The fourth was a sprint. A 91-yard LV drive from its own 9, capped by Tucker's 11-yard touchdown on third-and-8 (Q4 8:13, the game's biggest WPA swing at +0.211), tied it at 23. Buffalo needed 3:24 and seven plays to retake it, the decisive snap being Allen's third-and-3 checkdown to Cook for 26 to the LV 3. Bass's 38-yarder at 2:00 made it 33-23, and Tucker's 44-yard score — set up by a 22-yard DPI on Alford at 1:44 — made the final respectable without putting the ball back in Las Vegas's hands.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The divergence is almost entirely on the Raiders' side and almost entirely script. The engine blended LV to a 0.577 pass rate and 27.2 carries; the Raiders trailed on 60 of 66 snaps and finished at 0.672 and 20. That alone moves 60-plus passing yards and -35 rushing yards onto the board. The engine's own inputs — 2.99 YPC, 1.40 yards before contact, the worst in the file — already said this run game would be the first thing jettisoned; the volume projection just didn't act on it.

The second divergence is finishing. Ten red-zone trips produced five touchdowns and five field goals, and the field-versus-red-zone split tells the story on both sides: Buffalo 0.205 EPA/play on 51 field snaps against 0.114 on 14 red-zone snaps, Las Vegas 0.161 against -0.001. That is a yards-without-touchdowns game for both teams, and it is what let a 63-point total coexist with a one-possession margin.

On Buffalo's side the only real miss was Allen's legs: projected 35.6 rush yards, actual one. The Raiders' pressure was real — six QB hits, two sacks, a 41.3% blitz rate per charting — but it produced sacks rather than scrambles, and the short-yardage keeper never came.

The plan against the game

The plan's underdog mechanism was close to perfectly described and the favorite still won by it: Las Vegas chased, abandoned the run, reached 41 attempts, and Jeanty's carries withered to 10 for 22 while his receiving line landed within a yard and a half of projection. What broke was Buffalo's half. The plan read the Bills' 0.502 season pass rate as a lead property and expected a quiet passing box score with touchdowns on the ground; Buffalo led for 2,069 seconds and threw at 0.547 anyway, with an early-down pass rate (0.509) matching the expected rate exactly. That one error chained — Allen's rushing over, the pass-yards under and the pass-TD under all come off the same assumption. The Bowers miss is a different thing entirely: the role was exactly as described at nine targets and a 33.5% air-yard share, and he caught four, because Cousins completed 23 of 41 at -9.7 CPOE. That is the bounce, not the read.

Player by player

Cook was the model's best call and the game's most useful player: 22 carries for 95 with three ten-plus runs, plus 58 receiving yards on three targets at -8 air yards — 66 YAC — and the three-yard winner at 4:49. His receiving yardage (projected 5.7) is systematically underpriced against defenses that sit back. Coleman, with Moore out and Palmer at a 25% snap share, was the downfield alpha at 88 air yards and a 47.8% share. Kincaid led Buffalo in targets with eight but on 15 total air yards — receptions, not yards.

On the other side, Bowers kept his role and lost his yardage; Tucker (7-5-97-2, 108 air yards) and Mayer (9-7-95, 68 YAC) took the surplus. Mayer's nine targets against a 3.9 projection is the kind of secondary-tree spike a trailing script manufactures every time.

The defenses

Buffalo blitzed on 41.3% of LV snaps and generated zero sacks and a 14.6% pressure rate — below league. That is now the second game in two weeks where the pass rush disappeared, and the coverage behind it gave up four explosive passes (26, 28, 29, 44) and 33 DPI yards, two of which kept scoring drives alive. Las Vegas held Buffalo to 3.55 yards a carry on 29 attempts, which is better than its season allowance, but conceded three touchdowns on five trips and never got off the field on the two drives that mattered — Buffalo was 2-for-2 on the third downs at Q4 6:48 and 4:56.

The blueprint

To beat Buffalo you throw at it: four-plus explosives a week in all five games now, plus coverage flags that donate yardage. Las Vegas had the design and the targets and still fell short because its quarterback was 23-of-41 and its red-zone EPA was zero. A future opponent needs both — the vertical threat and the finishing. To beat Las Vegas you run at it and finish: 5.21 YPC allowed entering, 80% opponent red-zone TD rate, and the 'run game' tag in each of the last two weeks. Buffalo had the rushing efficiency to cash that and did, barely, with three stalled trips of its own.

What the model should take from this

Three things. Buffalo's run lean is not a lead property — price Allen at 32-34 attempts regardless of script. A trailing team's carry projection should be dragged toward its efficiency, not its season volume, when before-contact blocking is this bad. And red-zone splits from one game are nearly worthless as signal: Buffalo kicked twice partly because a holding penalty erased a touchdown, and Cousins' lone interception is the least persistent number in the file.
