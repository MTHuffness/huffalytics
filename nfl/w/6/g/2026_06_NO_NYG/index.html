The plan said

A near pick'em between two flawed teams: the Giants want to run at the league's softest run front, the Saints have to throw it with a quarterback playing on a broken hand. The backfield shake-up in New York is the biggest lever on the card.

A coin-flip game between a 1-4 team that cannot run and a 3-1-1 team that wants to. New York should lean on the ground early against a Saints front missing two starting linebackers and ranked near the bottom in rush success allowed, chewing clock and capping Jameis Winston's attempts. New Orleans answers the only way it can — short, high-volume passing at a Giants secondary down three defensive backs and prone to downfield flags. What happened

New Orleans 34, New York 27 — the Saints covered as 1.5-point favorites and the game sailed over 46.5 (61 points), but almost nothing about the projection's shape held: NO ran it 38 times for 175 and threw for just 144 while the Giants were forced into 47 attempts, 395 passing yards and three turnovers that turned a 10-10 game into 34-10.

The plan read the matchup's static facts correctly — no Giants pass rush, Giants pre-snap flags, a Saints front that gets run through — and then got the direction of the game exactly backwards. It expected New York to grind on the ground and cap Winston's attempts while New Orleans threw 35-40 times; the Giants threw 47 and ran 20, the Saints ran 38 and threw 30. The one path it drew that had a chance, the underdog path (NO early score, chasing Giants, Olave volume), inverted too: New Orleans was the favorite and won by running. Most of the call misses trace to that one inverted lean rather than to independent bad reads, though the Harris call was a reported-start that was never a role.

The script: The favorite won, but not the plan's favorite — New Orleans was laid 1.5 and the plan's 'favorite_path' was written for New York. The short version

New Orleans won 34-27 as a 1.5-point favorite and the game went over 46.5 by fourteen and a half points, and the projection still earned an F. The reason is that everything about the shape inverted. The model had the Saints throwing 34 times and running 25; they ran 38 times for 175 yards and threw for 144. It had the Giants running 29 times for 125; they ran 20 for 84 and dropped back 49. The side was right and the mechanism was unrecognizable.

How it unfolded

New York took the opening quarter on the back of a 12-play, 93-yard drive out of its own 7, converting a 4th-and-1 with a Winston quarterback sneak at 8:09 and hitting Likely for 24 before Tracy's 17-yard touchdown catch. Then the Giants' offense stopped functioning: four straight possessions of -6, 16, 29 and -5 net yards with three Winston sacks sprinkled through them, the pass rush the Saints weren't supposed to have arriving on 2nd-and-7 and 3rd-and-7 at the NYG 33.

The half turned on special teams. With 1:18 left and New York up 10-3, Charlie Jones fumbled the kickoff at the 27 and New Orleans recovered at the 28 — a 13-point WP swing. Shough scrambled 25 yards to the 3 on the next snap, Miller was stuffed for -1 on 3rd-and-1, and Shough found Lance on 4th-and-2 with five seconds left. Tied at the half, 10-10.

Out of the break New Orleans went seven plays and 69 yards, finished by a 31-yard catch-and-run to Kamara. From there the game was a sequence of Giants self-destruction: Winston intercepted by Emerson at the NO 26 (Q3 4:23), Winston intercepted by Blackmon and returned to the NYG 21 (Q4 12:28, which became an Olave touchdown two snaps later), McCarthy intercepted by Emerson at 10:27. 34-10, win probability at zero.

The last twelve minutes were a different game entirely and the reason the total cashed. McCarthy, 6-for-6 on deep throws with a 14.8 adot, pushed drives of 45, 73, 79 and 57 yards. The Giants scored 17 straight and got to 4th-and-1 at the NO 14 with twelve seconds left before an incompletion to Likely ended it.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The engine blended New Orleans to a 59% pass rate off a 64.8% season rate and gave them 4.12 yards a carry. What it could not model was a Saints team that, once it had the lead, threw on 30% of 30 snaps and leaned on an early-down pass rate of 34.5% against a 49.8% expectation. That is the most run-heavy posture of their season, and it worked: five rushes of 10-plus, 5.4 yards a carry to the right side, a 30:56 time of possession.

The mirror is the Giants. Blended to 28.6 carries, they got 38 of 70 snaps in a trailing state and threw on 78.9% of them. The 174.9-yard passing miss and the 15.3-attempt miss are one fact wearing two hats, compounded by a quarterback change that handed 21 dropbacks to a passer with a 14.8 air-depth who went 6-for-6 deep. That is noise. What was not noise: five sacks taken against 2.3 projected, Winston pressured on 30% of dropbacks while Shough faced one QB hit on 32 dropbacks against a 48.6% blitz rate. The pass-rush differential was in the inputs — NYG 12.8% pressure generated, 'no pass rush' tagged three times — and the model underweighted it.

The plan against the game

The plan got the static facts right and the direction wrong. It correctly identified the Saints' missing linebackers as the hole to run through and correctly identified the Giants as unable to rush the passer; it then assigned those facts to the wrong offenses. Both leans failed by wide margins: New Orleans called for pass played at 44.1%, New York called for run played at 72.2%, and pace called average came in fast at 140 plays. The most instructive miss is D3 — the plan's own reasoning said Miller only gets 20 carries in a game New Orleans leads, which is exactly the game that happened. And the U3 Harris call was a Sunday beat report that produced 14.3% of snaps.

Player by player

Nabers went 12-165 on 15 targets against a 65-yard projection, a 3.4x miss that was two-thirds comeback volume and one-third genuine target dominance (33.8% air-yards share). Likely hit his receptions projection exactly — 5.8 for 6 — while beating his yardage by 48 on throws McCarthy made in a two-score hole; his receiving EPA was -1.38 despite the box score. Miller's 20 carries for 70 came with -4.11 rushing EPA, pure clock-killing volume. Harris: 4 carries, 2 yards, 14.3% snaps. Juwan Johnson: 2 targets, zero catches on a 58.3% snap share, with Fant and Delp splitting the rest of the room — that one is a role read, not a bounce. Kamara at 26.4% snaps turned 11 touches into a 31-yard touchdown and 69 yards; the usage is low and the leverage is high.

The defenses

New Orleans forced three takeaways with Emerson (2 INTs, 4 PDs) and Blackmon, which they had not done all year, and gave up 7 explosive passes and 395 yards — the same bend-and-break pattern the week 3 and week 4 tags flagged, except the breaks came after the game was decided. The Giants' front produced nothing: zero sacks, one hit, a defense that got run on for 175 after being run on in weeks 2 and 4. Charting this season does not include coverage shells, so I'll stay on the proxies: a 48.6% blitz rate against New Orleans that generated a 3.1% hit rate is a blitz that isn't getting home.

What the model should take from this

Two real reads. First, New Orleans' pass rate is lead-contingent in a way the blend does not capture — shade it toward the run whenever they are favored. Second, their red-zone split points the right way: 73.3% TD rate and positive red-zone EPA against negative field EPA entering, 3-for-3 on nine snaps here. That is a team whose points beat its yards, which matters for team totals and anytime-TD props much more than for yardage lines.

Two things to leave alone. The kickoff fumble (-4.28 EPA, the game's largest WP swing) is special-teams variance. McCarthy's 6-for-6 deep in prevent coverage is garbage time. Neither should move a number next week — but the 25 garbage-time snaps that produced them are a reminder that a total can cash entirely after the side is dead.
