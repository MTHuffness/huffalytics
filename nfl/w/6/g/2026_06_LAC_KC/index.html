The read

The Chiefs' run game meets a Charger front whose low yards-per-carry number was earned against Arizona and Las Vegas and has since been gashed — 176 to Buffalo at 5.4, 138 to Seattle with 8.5 a pop on left-side carries — with Tomlinson and Caldwell both limited and Teair Tart rated in the bottom fifth of interior linemen. For Los Angeles, the Chiefs' front does not get home (21st in pressure generated, zero sacks in three of four games), so Herbert should see a cleaner pocket than usual — but Kansas City holds at its own 20 (4th in red-zone touchdown rate allowed), which turns Charger yards into field goals. The short version

Kansas City never trailed, never needed to throw a touchdown, and won 22-6 by handing the ball to Kenneth Walker 19 times for 128 yards and both scores. The Chargers' projected 17 points became six, and the gap was entirely at the goal line: three trips inside the 20, zero touchdowns, -0.965 EPA per snap in there, 5-of-17 on third down and 0-for-3 on fourth. KC -7.5 cashed, the under cashed by 13.5, and the engine's biggest single error (LAC points, score 0.45) was a finishing error, not a yardage error — Herbert's 204 passing yards landed within four of projection.

How it unfolded

KC's first two drives produced 128 yards and ten points, the second ending with Walker's 33-yard run on 3rd-and-1 at 7:29 of the first. From that point the Chargers were chasing on all 68 of their snaps. The one stretch where the game was live came in the second quarter: LAC moved to the KC 42, Herbert's pass for Lambert-Smith was picked by Cochrane and returned to the LAC 17 (-7.86 EPA, the biggest swing of the day), and then KC handed it straight back with Mahomes's interception from the LAC 2. From 9-3 KC went 80 yards in eight plays on a Kelce 24-yarder and a Walker goal-line run, and tacked on a 50-yard Butker kick with seven seconds left for 19-3.

The third quarter contained both teams' best and worst work. Johnston's 57-yard catch on 3rd-and-12 put LAC first-and-goal at the 1; a Slaughter holding penalty at 11:45 pushed them back and the drive produced three points. KC then ran seven plays to the LAC 1 and got stopped on 4th-and-1 (-3.98 EPA), giving LAC the ball at its own 1 — where a delay of game, a sack split by Woods and Jones, and a punt from the back of the end zone followed. Dye's forced fumble on the return handed KC the ball at the LAC 41. The fourth quarter was attrition: a missed 52-yarder, the Lambert-Smith fumble forced by Sneed at the KC 36, and an 11-play, 5:51 Chiefs field-goal drive to end it.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The volume projections were close to perfect on both sides — KC 30 attempts/27 carries against 30.7/28.8, LAC's 68 plays above a projected 61.6. What the engine could not price was the split at the 20. LAC's field EPA (-0.162) was bad but survivable; its red-zone EPA (-0.965) was catastrophic, and three trips produced six points. KC ran the inverse: 0.319 field EPA, -0.748 inside the 20, one touchdown on four trips, which is why 272 passing and 150 rushing yards came to only 22 points and the total still went under.

The other divergence was in the passing shape. KC's projected 224 yards became 272 on fewer attempts than projected, built on a 3.6 aDOT, +9.4 CPOE and 161 YAC — Worthy 7-for-7, Gray's 31-yard catch-and-run, Kelce's 24. LAC threw six more times than projected and gained less, at 5.4 aDOT with -9.9 CPOE and four sacks on 44 dropbacks.

The plan against the game

The pre-game plan's favorite path is almost a transcript of the first three quarters, and eight of its ten calls cashed, including the whole Chiefs-grind parlay. Its one real analytic error was its headline lever: it bet the Chargers would refuse to chase, and they threw on 65.1% of snaps against a 52.0% season rate. The error was harmless because the chase was all short — Herbert cleared the 199.5 yardage under by four yards on six extra attempts — but it should change how the next Charger plan is built: attempts up, air yards down. The Mitchell receptions miss was a role read, not a script read; the outlet work went to Vidal and a just-activated Lambert-Smith, exactly the risk the plan listed as an unknown.

The defenses

Charting carries box counts and blitz rates only, no coverage. KC faced a 20.8% blitz rate and a 6.31 average box and took zero sacks on 32 dropbacks; LAC faced 22.2% blitz and a 6.41 box and gave up 4 sacks and 8 QB hits on 44 dropbacks. That asymmetry, against an LAC line missing Alt and Awosika with a 10.9% season sack rate allowed, is the clearest pre-game edge in the dossier. KC's defense was the bend-don't-break unit it has been: it allowed 70 yards on the Johnston drive and three points, and 34 yards on the final drive and nothing.

The blueprint

To beat the Chargers you do not need an efficient offense — you need a back who hits explosive interior runs and a line that keeps you out of sack-fumble territory, because LAC has handed the ball over in four of five weeks and finished 0-for-3 in the red zone here. To beat the Chiefs you need something LAC did not have: 3+ explosive passes, field EPA above league, and the red-zone conversion to turn chunks into seven. LV did it (6 explosive passes, 27 points), IND did it (30). LAC produced one explosive play all day and kicked.

What the model should take from this

Two entries. First, the Charger trailing-lean input is now in doubt — one game is one observation, but a 13-point gap between actual and season pass rate with early-down rate still below expectation says the right model is high attempts at suppressed depth. Second, do not shade KC's team total down for a 1-for-4 red-zone day, a stuffed 4th-and-1 and a missed 52-yarder; the field efficiency (0.319 EPA/play) was fine and the finishing is the least persistent number in the dossier.
