Arizona won 18-16 at SoFi on five Cade Ryland field goals and a last-second 43-yarder, covering +12.5 and cashing the under by 17.5 — the Rams never led by more than three, lost the possession battle 34:52 to 25:08, and the projection's only big team-level error was 32 implied points for a Los Angeles offense that ran 57 plays under pressure.

The plan found the correct path — it wrote the underdog route almost exactly (long dink drives, time of possession, Rams giveaways) and then bet against it, leaning on a 50-attempt Stafford script that required Los Angeles to be in front. What it missed was the clause it wrote itself — Arizona won precisely by stacking Ryland field goals rather than touchdowns, so the underdog cashed while failing the plan's stated requirement. The short version

Arizona went to SoFi a 12.5-point underdog, never trailed until 47 seconds remained, and won 18-16 on Chad Ryland's fifth and sixth field goals — a 27-yarder in the fourth and a 43-yarder at 0:03. The under (51.5) cashed by 17.5 points. The projection had Arizona almost exactly right (19.5 points, 206 pass yards, 33 attempts against 18/218/35) and Los Angeles wildly high: 32 implied points for an offense that got 57 snaps, absorbed a 27% pressure rate, and left three possessions inside Arizona's 30 without points.

How it unfolded

The first quarter looked like the market's game. Brissett's third-and-5 throw for McBride was tipped by Fiske and picked by Quentin Lake at the ARI 32 (12:23), and the Rams converted the short field into a 44-yard Grupe kick. Then Arizona discovered it could hold the ball. Brissett found McBride for 26 on the next drive (8:57), Bam Knight ripped 31 off left end to the LA 10 (1:04), and the pattern was set: Arizona moved, stalled, kicked. Corum's 15-yard reception at Q2 8:29 was the Rams' only lead of the first three quarters.

The third quarter was the giveaway. Arizona burned 8:35 on a 15-play, 66-yard field-goal drive; the Rams answered by reaching the ARI 26 and handing Davis Allen the ball on 4th-and-1 into Gibbens and Mack Wilson (-4.00 EPA). Arizona then ate another 8:11 and kicked again for 15-9. Los Angeles's next three drives: a 26-yard DPI gift followed by a strip-sack recovery and then a Higbee fumble forced by Calais Simon at the ARI 22; a false start and a 4th-and-8 incompletion at the ARI 44; and finally the 91-yard, eight-play drive that worked — Nacua for 43, Nacua for 16, Parkinson for the score on review at 0:52.

That touchdown put LA up one with 47 seconds and Arizona holding timeouts. Brissett threw deep middle to Jalen Brooks for 21, Josh Wallace added 15 for unnecessary roughness (+2.86 EPA, +0.241 WP), and the rest was arithmetic.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The engine's error was volume, not talent. It blended Arizona to 61.6 plays and Los Angeles to 63.0; the actuals were 72 and 57, a 15-snap swing driven entirely by Arizona never trailing. Arizona's 62.9% season pass rate became 48.6% — 44% even in the handful of snaps they were behind. Los Angeles's pass rate held at 63.2%, so the Rams' problem was never lean; it was that 37 dropbacks is all you get when the opponent possesses the ball for 34:52 and you give three drives away.

The second error was sack rate. The projection carried LA at a 5.5% sack rate against a defense tagged "no pass rush" in three of five weeks. Arizona blitzed 37.8% (charting has blitz and box; coverage is not charted this season), producing three sacks and seven hits against a patched left tackle. Pressure is why LA's yards came at 4.8 air yards per throw and why a 25-of-33, +4.2 CPOE line produced 222 yards.

The plan against the game

The plan wrote Arizona's winning path in its own underdog paragraph — long dink drives, time of possession, Rams giveaways — and then bet the other side of it, hanging everything on Stafford reaching 38-42 attempts. The one factor it correctly named as a hidden risk, Quessenberry at left tackle, is the factor that broke the whole passing slate. Its Arizona-side reads were excellent (McBride catches over, yards under, Brissett yards under all swept on exactly the stated mechanism), and its Rams-side reads failed on quantity rather than logic. The caveat it attached to Arizona — that they'd need touchdowns instead of Ryland field goals — turned out to be wrong in the most instructive way: they went 0-for-5 inside the 20 and won anyway.

Player by player

Bam Knight (15-80) against a 2.3-carry projection is the week's lesson on stale usage shares: with Love out, the model handed the backfield to Allgeier, who took 18 carries for 38 with four stuffs behind a line averaging 2.11 yards before contact. Kendrick Bourne doubled his projected targets (8) and was the chain-mover on both long third-quarter drives. On the other side, Colby Parkinson (6 targets, 5-31, the go-ahead score) took the tight-end role the plan had assigned entirely to Higbee, who finished with 22 yards and the fumble. Nacua's targets were projected to the number (7) and still produced only 80 yards because the attempt pool shrank; Kyren Williams lost his goal-line equity to a Corum reception and split carries 10-9 with him.

The defenses

Arizona's secondary, beaten over the top in four straight weeks, faced four deep attempts all day and allowed one explosive — because the Rams never asked. Where Arizona won was sending five: 3 sacks, 7 hits, two stops on fourth down. The Rams' defense was the better unit by EPA, surrendering 72 snaps and 34:52 of clock while giving up zero touchdowns on five red-zone trips (ARI -0.606 EPA/play inside the 20 against +0.139 outside). That is bend-don't-break — except LA had allowed 1.00, 0.80 and 1.00 red-zone touchdown rates in weeks 3-5, so it is a one-week reversal, not a trait.

What the model should take from this

Two things repeat: Arizona's offense moves between the 20s and kicks, and Los Angeles loses possessions it has already earned (turnovers tagged in four of five weeks, five-plus pre-snap flags in two). One thing does not: the red-zone split in this game, on both sides, ran against every prior week and should not move the touchdown constant. The structural fix is the volume model — when a favorite's implied points require 63 snaps and the dog runs 66+ per game and leans run with a lead, the favorite's team total and its quarterback's attempt line are both overpriced before anything happens on the field.
