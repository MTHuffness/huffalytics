The read

Indianapolis by a touchdown over a Tennessee team that has scored 10, 20, 7, 18 and 3 and runs the fewest plays in the league (53.4/game). The Colts don't have to be good to win this — they have to stay ahead and hand it off. Tennessee's defense is 30th in pass EPA allowed and 28th in pressure generated, which is the one place Cam Ward's low-aDOT quick game can actually function; the problem is that Tennessee is 4th in yards per carry allowed and 4th in red-zone EPA allowed, so this is a game where Indianapolis moves the ball between the 20s and may well cash it in field goals and short runs rather than chunk drives. Expect Tennessee trailing from the second quarter on, Ward at 33-36 attempts, and Jonathan Taylor at 20+ touches with his yardage grinding rather than exploding.

Why

The Colts' own profile is run-first when ahead: 42.9% second-half pass rate in the Week 4 win at Washington, 54.5% overall, three rushing touchdowns and 143 passing yards. Tennessee's run defense allows 3.54 yards a carry (4th) but 114 rushing yards a game — volume without efficiency, which is exactly how a lead-protecting offense uses it. On the other side, Indianapolis generates pressure at 12.9% (24th) with Tuimoloau in concussion evaluation, and has allowed 268 passing yards a game (28th). Ward's chase volume has been real in every game he trailed (32, 36, 31, 36 attempts), and Carnell Tate is the designated air-yards winner (73.1% of them in Week 4).

What would change it

Tyler Warren's groin — ESPN's Fowler had him a game-time decision Sunday morning. Out, and the Colts' targets scatter to Downs, Treadwell and Allen and the red-zone menu narrows to Taylor. The short version

Indianapolis gained 434 yards on 82 snaps and lost by six. Tennessee gained 186 on 45, threw for 102, and scored 26. That is a game decided entirely by the margins: three Colts turnovers, six sacks of Daniel Jones, a blocked punt returned for a touchdown, and Joey Slye going 4-for-4 including a 56. The model's spread call (TEN +6.5) cashed; its winner and its Under did not, and the projection graded out a D at 0.525 largely because the volume shape of the game inverted — Tennessee was supposed to throw 32 times chasing and threw 22 while tied.

How it unfolded

Indianapolis opened with a 14-play, 77-yard march and finished it with an interception at the Tennessee 6. That play set the day's grammar. The Colts' next drive produced the game's only sustained touchdown — Jones's 38-yard throw to Taylor (Q2 14:34) and a one-yard score to Alie-Cox — and after that the offense alternated between first downs and self-inflicted wounds: three sacks before halftime, a 13-play, 70-yard drive that ended in a 23-yard field goal after Johnson's nine-yard sack at Q2 3:39.

Tennessee trailed 13-0 at the half having produced 17 plays and -0.386 EPA per play. Then the field reversed. Simmons punched the ball out of Taylor's hands on the first snap of the third quarter, giving the Titans the ball at the IND 33 (field goal); Flott intercepted Jones at the Tennessee 11 to end a 12-play drive; Tennessee's 51-yard answer ended in a 56-yard kick. At 13-6 the Titans took over at the IND 28 and Ward hit Tate for the tying score. Three minutes later Chestnut blocked Sanchez's punt and Oladejo returned it 14 yards — 0.279 of win probability in one play, and Tennessee's lead.

Indianapolis answered with its best drive of the day (8 plays, 72 yards, Taylor from the 4). Tennessee's winning possession started at its own 17 and lived on a 36-yard pass interference call on Taylor-Britt at Q4 5:26 — free yards that appear nowhere in Ward's passing line — before Slye's 49-yarder. A Johnson sack on third-and-10 and a 4th-and-17 incompletion ended it.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The engine built Tennessee's line off a 0.572 blended pass rate, which assumed chase mode. Tennessee was tied or leading for 1,908 of 3,600 seconds and threw 45% while tied and 0% while leading; the result was 22 attempts and 102 yards against a 222 projection. That is script, not projection error on rate — but the efficiency failed too: -20.1 CPOE in a pocket that produced zero sacks and one QB hit. On the other side the Colts were given 62 plays and ran 82, because their drives kept extending without ending. Rush yards beat the projection by 57 (5.5 a carry, 7.3 right side) while rushing EPA was -0.192 — movement, not value. The 7.5-point miss on Tennessee's total is mostly non-offensive: 13 of 26 points came from the blocked punt and two kicks of 44-plus.

The plan against the game

The plan wrote the underdog path almost correctly and for the right reason — Jones gives it away under pressure, Tennessee runs early against a soft front, a 20-23 point game the Titans win late — and then bet the half of it that failed. It needed Ward's quick game to turn 35 attempts into 240 yards; Tennessee never trailed enough to produce attempts, and checks shows both leans inverted (IND 61.5% pass against a 57.3% season rate, TEN 48.9% against 59.6%). Pace was called right at 123 total plays. The Warren swing factor landed but redistributed to Allen and Mallory rather than Treadwell, which is why D1 missed by 42 yards.

Player by player

Tate was the one Titan who was bet-able regardless of volume: 7 targets, 6-70-1, 64.9% of the team's air yards on a 22-attempt day. Pollard's 19/76 matched the projection almost exactly. Ward's line is a warning — a clean pocket does not fix accuracy. For Indianapolis, Allen is the story: 11 targets, 8-82, 34 YAC, 71% of snaps with Warren out; Mallory's six targets on 52% of snaps are the other half of that vacancy. Taylor's 21/94 plus a 38-yard catch-and-run inflated his receiving line on one play, and his touchdown came from the 4 — the role, not the yardage, is what repeats.

The defenses

Tennessee generated a 22% pressure rate (6 sacks, 11 hits) against a season rate of 11.7%, almost all of it from four rushers against a 26.9% blitz rate. Simmons alone had two sacks, two TFL and the forced fumble. That is a line-matchup result — the Colts gave up four sacks to Houston in Week 3 — more than a Titans trait. Indianapolis, meanwhile, did not touch Ward (4.3% pressure) and still held him to 102 yards, because Tennessee only needed 23 dropbacks. The Colts held at their own 20 when it mattered less than usual; Tennessee's defense allowed 0.145-type field movement all day and then took the ball away three times in scoring territory.

The blueprint

Indianapolis gets beaten by itself — three turnover-tagged weeks out of five before this one, red-zone stalls in Weeks 3 and 5, a line that folds against interior rush — and it gets beaten on the ground, with the run-game tag in Weeks 1, 2 and 5. Tennessee gets beaten by explosive passing (three weeks tagged) and by its own inability to sustain: 53 snaps a game, 0.40 red-zone touchdown rate, -0.158 field EPA. Indianapolis tried the blueprint and had the yardage to cash it; it could not finish, and that is the whole six points.

What the model should take from this

Two real reads: Jones's volume rises with plays rather than falling with a lead, and Allen is the primary short-area target when Warren is out. Two bounces not to price in: the blocked punt and the 36-yard DPI. And one structural correction — when an underdog's path to covering runs through takeaways and special teams rather than offense, its QB's attempt and yardage projections should come down, not up.
