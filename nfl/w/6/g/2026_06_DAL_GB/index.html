What happened

Green Bay 23, Dallas 13: the Packers covered as 1.5-point home dogs and the 53.5 total died by 17.5 points, because Dallas moved the ball for 77 plays and gave three scoring-position possessions back (two Prescott interceptions inside the GB 20, two turnovers on downs) while Green Bay turned five explosive throws and a 37-yard DPI into a 17-0 halftime lead.

The plan's picture of Green Bay was broadly right and its picture of Dallas was wrong in the way that mattered. It correctly identified that GB has to throw, that Golden is the target-floor play with Reed and Moore out, and that Dallas can't pressure — GB hit five explosives and led wire-to-wire. The read

This is a shootout with asymmetric shapes. Dallas's defense is last in EPA allowed, last in field EPA allowed (between the 20s), 32nd in rushing yards allowed per game and 29th in pressure generated — with Overshown out a fifth straight week and Durant out, Porter questionable. Green Bay gets the cleanest pocket and the softest field of any week this season, and Love has thrown exactly two touchdowns in all five games. Green Bay's own defense is the mirror image: 5th in field EPA allowed and 5th in passing yards allowed per game, but 29th in red-zone touchdown rate allowed (0.786) and 32nd in red-zone EPA. Dallas should find the yards slow and the trips profitable.

Why

The split at the 20 carries this plan. Dallas's passing yardage is the half Green Bay actually defends (6.35 yards per attempt allowed, 8th), which argues down on Prescott's yardage and on the receiving lines beneath him — and Dallas no longer needs 42 throws, because Green Bay's interior line is gutted (Hargrave to IR, Ford to IR, Campbell questionable, Wyatt limited). That is the same hole Atlanta ran through for 242 in week 3. Once Dallas reaches the 20, Green Bay's finishing rank hands the points over, and Javonte Williams has scored in four of five games at a 74-85% snap share.

Green Bay's path is simpler: Dallas has allowed four or more explosive passes in four straight weeks and generated almost no pressure in any of them. Golden and Watson have held 7-12 and 8-11 target weeks with Reed and Moore both on IR; the WR3 job falls to a just-activated Savion Williams.

What would change it

Cisse's hip (Packers CB1, every snap last week) and Porter's ankle for Dallas — both decide how clean each passing game is. Green Bay leading comfortably is the known trap: our week-4 review showed this offense runs at 47% when it's ahead, which kills Love's volume. And if Dallas trails by two scores again, its carries collapse toward the teens as they did against Tampa.

The short version

Green Bay 23, Dallas 13: the home underdog covered a 1.5-point line and a 53.5 total finished 17.5 points short. Dallas ran 77 plays, threw 54 times and generated 258 passing yards, and still scored 13 — because the three possessions that reached Green Bay territory in the first half ended at the GB 3, the GB 18 and the half, and the two that reached it in the second half ended on downs. Green Bay, an offense that cannot run a step (26 carries, 57 yards, 0.4 ypc up the middle), scored on explosives and field goals and never trailed.

How it unfolded

The first two drives set the terms. Dallas went six plays for 19 and punted; Green Bay went 15 plays and 105 yards, converting 3rd-and-5, 3rd-and-13 (Watson, 27 yards, +3.47 EPA), 3rd-and-13 again and 3rd-and-6 for Golden's 9-yard touchdown at 4:46. Dallas got one gift — Winters picked Love at the GB 42 at 1:53, a return touchdown wiped out on review — and turned a start at the opponent's 42 into Prescott's interception at the GB 3 (Q2 14:23, -4.30 EPA). Green Bay then burned 8:17 off the clock for a 25-yard field goal, and after a third Dallas drive died on Cisse's pick at the GB 18 (Q2 2:00, -3.96), Love worked 39 yards in 1:27 — 37 of them on a pass-interference flag against Porter on an incompletion — and sneaked it in at 0:25. 17-0, and Green Bay's win probability never dipped below 37.8% all night.

The second half was Dallas doing enough to stay on the field and not enough to score. A 4th-and-2 conversion to Lamb for 17 (+4.00) produced the only real touchdown drive; Green Bay answered with its own 4th-and-3 conversion to Kraft (+3.57) and a 44-yard field goal. From there Dallas went 0-for-2 on fourth downs — midfield at Q4 13:33, and 4th-and-11 at the GB 14 at 5:05 after an 18-play, 5:06 march that gained 43 yards. The 6-yard touchdown to Flournoy with 52 seconds left was the 13th point.

Why the game diverged from the projection

The grade is a C for a specific reason: the player distribution was fine (0.767) and the team volume was wrong (0.524). The engine blended Dallas to 62 plays at a 57.2% pass rate and got 77 at 72.7%, with 15 carries against a 26.6 projection — all of it downstream of trailing on 71 of 77 snaps. That isn't purely noise: the week-5 'abandoned the run' tag says this team dumps the ground game the moment it's behind, and the trailing pass rate (74.6%) matched that tag almost exactly.

The points miss is a different animal. Dallas didn't fail to move — 258 passing yards, four red-zone trips — it failed to finish and it gave the ball away twice in scoring position under a 12.5% pressure rate. That is coverage, not pass rush: Cisse, Evan Williams and Bullard combined for six pass breakups and two picks, and Prescott's -3.1 cpoe on a 6.9 aDOT is a quarterback throwing short into a defense that was comfortable. Green Bay's rushing miss (110.3 projected, 57 actual) was the cleanest signal in the file: a 3.05-ypc team met its own ceiling again.

The plan against the game

The plan read Green Bay correctly and Dallas backwards. It saw the explosive-pass path over a secondary with no rush in front of it, and it named Golden as the target-floor play with Reed and Moore out — 13 targets, 104 yards, 52% of the air yards. What it could not have from the box score was a Dallas offense that would throw 54 times in a game it was supposed to grind, and five of its seven misses were volume calls priced on the absence of a chase script that arrived at 4:46 of the first quarter. The Prescott under cashed anyway, on depth and completion rate rather than on carries, and the Lloyd call failed for a reason nothing in the projection anticipated: Pierre Strong took the backfield.

Player by player

Lamb's 18 targets against a 7.1 projection is the headline, but his 70 receiving yards against 61.6 is the lesson — the targets went up 150% and the yards went up 14%, because his average target was short and two of them were intercepted (-14.28 EPA across his looks). Pickens cleared 69.5 at 73 on 11 targets, Flournoy went 7-40 and the late score on 87.2% of snaps, Spann-Ford out-targeted Ferguson 5-3. On the other side, Golden and Watson combined for 21 targets and 348 air yards, Kraft hit 37 yards on the number, and the backfield was Strong's: 11 carries, 6 targets, 54.7% of snaps against Lloyd's 40% and Johnson's single carry for -2 yards. Savion Williams, activated Saturday and unprojected, played 53% of snaps for 4-32 and two carries.

The defenses

Dallas's front actually showed up more than its inputs suggested — three sacks on Love (Quinnen Williams, Garcia, Clark's 10-yarder at the GB 4) and eight QB hits, an 18.2% pressure rate against a 3.4% opponent sack-rate input. What it could not do, again, was keep the top on: five 20+ passes, 4-of-11 deep for 113. Green Bay's defense did the opposite — no takeaway-free bend, just 4.8 yards per attempt allowed on 54 throws, six breakups, two picks, and a red zone that gave up two touchdowns but took the ball twice before Dallas got there. Charting is formation-only this season: Dallas faced a 35.5% blitz rate and 4.53 rushers, Green Bay 32.7% and 4.41 — neither defense needed exotic pressure.

What the model should take from this

Three things. First, volume projections on Dallas should be conditioned on the first-quarter scoreboard, not on the pregame spread; this offense's play count and pass rate swing 15 points on game state. Second, Green Bay's rushing inputs are not a matchup variable — 3.05 ypc entering, 2.19 here, worst YBC in the file — so a Packers rush projection near 110 yards should be shaded down against anyone. Third, red-zone and takeaway variance did the scoring work here: 140 combined plays produced 36 points. When both offenses carry sub-0.60 red-zone finishing and one defense forces picks at a 4.5% clip, pace is an argument for the under, not the over.
