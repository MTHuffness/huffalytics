The plan said

Atlanta runs at a Chicago front missing Sweat, Odeyingbo and Edwards; Chicago's returning quarterback meets the league's No. 5 pressure defense. The lever is whether the Falcons get the early lead that kills their own pass rate.

Atlanta should get the matchup it wants early: Bijan Robinson and Brian Robinson running at a Bears front missing its two best edge rushers and its starting linebacker. Chicago answers with pace — it runs 72.8 plays a game and holds the ball — but its yards come between the 20s and stall inside them. What happened

Chicago led wire to wire and won 31-23, covering -1.5 while the game went over 51.5 on 16 Atlanta points after the Bears led 31-7; the projection's team totals were close but its biggest input — 27 Atlanta carries — died the moment the Falcons trailed by 14 in the first quarter.

The plan picked the right side of the central question — it named the lever as 'whether the Falcons get the early lead that kills their own pass rate' — and the answer was no, which decided everything. But it had the mechanism pointed the wrong way: it expected Atlanta to be the team that ran and led, and it expected Chicago's passing to shrink under pressure. Chicago won by being the efficient, lead-holding team the plan's favorite path described, except the quick game produced 305 yards instead of a short-yardage grind, and the red-zone conversion the plan treated as an if came in at 4-of-5. The Bijan calls were not a bad read of the matchup, they were a bad read of script probability; the Loveland and Williams unders were read errors about where a 3.9-aDOT offense's yards go.

The script: The favorite path called for Chicago winning the snap count, moving it with the quick game and converting the red-zone trips it had been wasting, for roughly 27-20 — it won the snap count 76-53, ran a 3.9 aDOT, went 4-of-5 inside the 20 and won 31-23. But two pieces of the stated mechanism failed: Williams was not kept clean (4 sacks, 7 hits, 17.1% pressure) and Odunze was not the engine (4 targets, 39 yards) — Burden and Loveland were. Checks confirm the pace call (123 plays vs 121.5 league) and show both lean calls broke: Chicago passed at .548 against a 'balanced' call and Atlanta at .760 against a 'run' call, because it trailed on all 53 snaps.

The short version

Chicago 31, Atlanta 23 — a one-score final that was never a one-score game. The Bears led 14-0 after eleven minutes, 31-7 at the start of the fourth quarter, and surrendered 16 points in the last ten minutes to a no-huddle offense that was already beaten. CHI -1.5 cashed, the 51.5 went over on garbage-time touchdowns and two successful two-point conversions, and the projection's single biggest error — Atlanta at 27.1 carries, actual 12 — was a script failure, not a matchup failure.

How it unfolded

Chicago's opening drive answered the pre-game question in six minutes: eleven plays, 87 yards, two third-down conversions (the Odunze catch-and-lateral on 3rd-and-11, EPA +3.01, and the 14-yard Loveland touchdown on 2nd-and-13). The second drive needed a 23-yard Odunze completion on 3rd-and-17 and ended in a Swift touchdown. Atlanta's first two possessions produced three yards on six plays.

From there the Falcons' day was a sequence of near-misses in bad field position. A 39-yard London catch set up the Dotson touchdown that made it 14-7, and then the game's biggest single swing arrived: Taylor's punt with 1:52 left in the half, Branch muffing it at the ATL 10, Blackwell recovering (+5.07 EPA), Swift scoring three plays later for 21-7. Atlanta's next five possessions: turnover on downs (the 4th-and-3 incompletion to London at the CHI 28, -3.34 EPA), punt after a Gordon strip-sack at its own 2, a Branch kickoff fumble that became a 50-yard field goal, and another turnover on downs. Three of Atlanta's four fourth-quarter touchdown drives came at 31-7 or worse; the only one that mattered followed Roschon Johnson's fumble at the ATL 45 (Q4 7:21, -5.58 EPA). Gordon's second strip-sack at 31-23 with 3:01 left ended it.

Why the game diverged from the projection

Two components: Atlanta's carries scored a flat 0.00 and its rushing yards 0.40. Atlanta trailed on all 53 of its snaps, ran a .736 pass rate against a .464 season rate, and went 77.4% shotgun with 18.9% no-huddle. That is the engine blending a lead-script pass rate into a game the team never led. Everything downstream — Bijan at 9 carries, Brian Robinson at 2, London at 11 targets instead of 8.5 — follows from it.

Chicago's 305 passing yards against 235.6 is the more interesting miss because the volume was right (36 attempts vs 35.1). The yardage came from a completion rate the inputs had no way to carry: 30-of-36, +15.3 CPOE, 3.9 aDOT, 193 of 305 yards after the catch. Atlanta's front did its job — 2.97 yards per Bears carry, 4 sacks, 7 hits — and the Bears simply threw it out of their hands. The other distortion is the red zone: a team tagged for red-zone stalls three weeks running went 4-for-5 with +0.558 EPA/play inside the 20 while posting -0.152 EPA/play on its other 59 snaps. That is the inverse of its season profile and the reason 403 total yards turned into 31 points.

The plan against the game

The plan named the right lever — whether Atlanta got the early lead that kills its own pass rate — and then bet the wrong side of it. Its favorite path (snap-count edge, quick game, red-zone conversion, roughly 27-20) is essentially what happened, but the two mechanisms it leaned on inside that path both broke: Williams was hit seven times and threw for 305 anyway, and Odunze, the designated downfield share, got four targets while Burden and Loveland took fifteen. The Bijan calls were a defensible matchup read drowned by script probability; the Loveland and Williams passing unders were read errors about where a 3.9-aDOT offense puts its yards. Checks: pace called average and played average (123 plays vs 121.5), both lean calls failed.

Player by player

Burden caught all eight targets for 95 on a 39% snap share — the clearest per-snap mispricing on the card, and repeatable in this offense. Loveland went 6-65 and a touchdown on 88.3% of snaps with 27.5% of the air yards; the under on him was built on a premise about his role that the tape contradicts. Odunze led Chicago in air yards and air-yards share (38, 31.7%) with +6.63 receiving EPA and still missed 39.5 by half a yard — a four-target alpha. Roschon Johnson's 13 carries against 2.9 projected is the Monangai vacancy landing somewhere the model didn't look.

On the Atlanta side, Penix's passing yards (225 vs 221.4) were the single best call in the file and arrived by an entirely wrong route: 33 attempts and five sacks rather than 25 attempts with a lead. His three touchdowns are red-zone-only output (3-for-3, +0.599 EPA/play) bolted onto -0.325 EPA/dropback. Pitts and Dotson both tripled their projections in the fourth quarter at 31-7 and 31-15; London took 44% of the air yards for four catches and -2.27 EPA.

The defenses

Chicago's, without Odeyingbo, held Atlanta to 32.1% success and -0.473 EPA/play between the 20s, forced 5 sacks and 3 fumbles, and stopped three of three fourth downs. Gordon, at 22% of snaps, had two strip-sacks and three tackles for loss. What it gave up were chunks: 33, 29, 41 and 39 yards — the same facet as six explosives allowed at Carolina and six at Green Bay. Charting shows Atlanta faced a 52.4% blitz rate and 4.79 average rushers, so the pressure was manufactured.

Atlanta's front held Chicago to 2.97 a carry and 5 stuffs with only a 22.2% blitz rate and 4.2 rushers against 76 snaps — and then conceded four touchdowns on five red-zone trips, which is now three straight weeks of giving up 75%+ inside the 20.

The blueprint

Beat Atlanta by scoring on your first two possessions without needing explosives; the carries disappear, Penix goes to shotgun and the strip-sacks follow. Beat Chicago by holding up in protection long enough to hit one chunk per drive, and by winning the turnover column — its own giveaways are tagged in three of five weeks. Neither team was capable of executing the other's blueprint today: Atlanta had the Y/A (7.77 season) and never got a neutral script to use it.

What the model should take from this

Treat Atlanta's run lean as conditional on the scoreboard and branch it. Model Chicago's passing off aDOT and CPOE rather than opponent pressure rate. And leave the finishing splits alone — 8 red-zone trips between the two teams is noise, and Chicago's 133 penalty yards carried only two pre-snap flags, which is the only part that persists.
