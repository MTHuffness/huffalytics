The short version

Seattle ran 82 plays to Denver's 59, out-gained the Broncos by more than 200 yards, and lost in overtime because it reached the red zone once. Three giveaways — Shaheed's fumble at the Denver 30, Darnold's interception on 3rd-and-1 inside field-goal range at 1:31, and the overtime pick that handed Denver the ball at the Seattle 43 — plus a missed 47-yard field goal and five sacks for 42 yards made 391 yards worth fourteen points. Denver covered as a 2.5-point home underdog and the total stayed under 40.5 despite 139 plays and an extra period.

How it unfolded

Seattle opened by moving the ball and immediately paying for it: back-to-back Darnold sacks by McMillian and Robinson at Q1 11:31 and 10:49 cost -3.8 EPA and turned a drive into a punt, and the second possession ended in a 55-yard Myers kick. Denver's answer was the game's only real sustained drive — 17 plays, 79 yards, 8:39, kept alive by Badie's six-yard fourth-down run at the 48 (+2.9 EPA) and an Engram conversion on 3rd-and-8 — and Dobbins scored from the four.

The second touchdown was gifted. On 3rd-and-10 from the Denver 21, Byron Murphy's face mask turned an eight-yard completion into a 23-yard gain, and Witherspoon's 20-yard DPI at 5:19 put the Broncos at the goal line for Harvey's direct-snap score. Thirty-five of the drive's 44 yards came from Seattle's secondary. Seattle answered by driving to the Denver 30 and having Bonitto punch the ball out of Shaheed's hands.

The second half was Denver doing essentially nothing — drives of 9, 4, 3, 23, -10 and 2 yards, -0.561 EPA per play — and Seattle failing to convert the field it owned. Shaheed's 42-yard catch set up the missed 47-yarder; sacks by Elliss and the Allen/Bonitto pair ended two more possessions. The 12-play, 66-yard touchdown drive at Q4 9:18, capped by Holani and a two-point conversion to Shaheed, made it 14-11, and after the Abrams-Draine interception Shaheed's 31-yard punt return set up Myers's 34-yard tie at :07. In overtime McMillian picked Darnold at the Seattle 43 and Lutz hit from 53.

Why the game diverged from the projection

Two volume assumptions broke. Seattle was modeled for 31.2 attempts on a 54.3% blended pass rate; it threw 51 times at a 68.3% rate because it trailed on 63 of 82 snaps and played overtime. That is script, not philosophy — its early-down pass rate (60.9%) was below the expected 67.6%. Denver was modeled for 31.6 attempts and 198 passing yards and threw 27 for 107, because it led for 2,573 seconds and because Nix's 3.0 aDOT and 0-for-3 deep line are what his 5.82 season Y/A looks like against a defense allowing 5.45. That one is signal: the engine's 6.27 blended yards per attempt was too kind to a short-area passer.

The points, though, were close on both sides (DEN 17 vs 19.0, SEA 14 vs 21.5) and the total projection of 40.5 beat the market's own number. Seattle's shortfall is a finishing story, not a movement story — field EPA -0.135, one red-zone trip, a 0.728 EPA/play inside the 20 when it finally got there.

The plan against the game

The plan's Denver side was close to perfect and for the stated reasons: Nix capped at 107, Waddle and Sutton combined for 43 on their air-yard shares, Dobbins at 2.4 a carry. Its Seattle side rested on a lead that never came, so the run-lean call inverted (68.3% pass), the pace call inverted (139 plays against a 121.5 league baseline), Wilson's and Holani's touches never arrived, and the Kupp volume fade died in a 51-attempt game. Most tellingly, the underdog path it wrote — Darnold gives it away, Denver gets a short field, converts its red-zone trips, final near 20-19 — is the game that was played. The plan saw the losing branch; it just bet the other one.

Player by player

Smith-Njigba is the durable lesson: 16 targets, 183 air yards, 43.5% of the team's air-yard share, 12-134 with no touchdown. The concentration survives any script. Kupp's 10 targets and Horton's five are the opposite — pure chase volume, with -0.11 and -2.95 receiving EPA respectively. Wilson hit his projected rushing yardage almost exactly (52 on 51.6) but on a 31.8% snap share; Holani played 57.6% of snaps, took five carries, drew zero targets on 51 attempts and still scored. That backfield is a committee with unpredictable touch distribution, and Velus Jones's four unprojected carries make it worse. For Denver, Harvey was the efficient back (+2.34 rush EPA, 38 YAC on negative air yards) at 40% of snaps while Dobbins ground out 17 carries for -4.75 EPA.

The defenses

Seattle's front and coverage were the better unit all night: 74 Denver rushing yards on 28 carries, 3-of-14 on third down, -0.174 field EPA, Emmanwori with a sack and three pressures, Ernest Jones with nine tackles and a sack. It gave up points only where it has all season — at the goal line and through its own flags. Denver's pass rush won without exotics: 33.3% blitz rate per charting, 6.24-man boxes, and Robinson and Roach generating four pressures each, with four of five sacks on third down. Its corners, down Surtain and Hufanga, allowed yards (Barron 67, Brandon Jones 78 with three missed tackles) but produced the two interceptions that decided it.

What the model should take from this

Seattle's trailing-script pass rate is the operative input, not its season rate. Denver should be modeled run-lean whenever it is not behind. And the engine's yards-per-attempt blend needs more weight on quarterback aDOT when facing top-tier coverage: a 3-to-4 aDOT passer against a sub-5.6 Y/A defense is a structural under, not a variance play. The red-zone split — Seattle's opponents now 7-for-7 over three weeks — is worth tracking but not yet worth a model constant; the trip counts that produce it are small-sample noise.
